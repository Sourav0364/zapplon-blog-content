## What a Google Business Profile costs and how long setup takes

A Google Business Profile is **free**. Google does not charge to create, verify or run one, and that is the same in California as anywhere else in the US. The only costs are your time and, if you hire help, the person doing the work.

Expect **30 to 60 minutes** to fill in a complete profile if you have your photos, hours and service list ready. Verification is the part you do not control, and it can add anything from a few minutes (video or phone) to several days (postcard). The profile is what shows your business on Google Maps and in the "map pack" when someone nearby searches for what you sell.

If you only do one thing for local visibility this year, do this. It is the cheapest lead source you have, and it sits alongside the wider [local search and lead generation services](https://www.zapplon.com/services) a growing business may eventually add.

## Before you start: what to have ready

Gather these first. It stops you abandoning the form halfway and leaving a half-built profile live.

- **Exact business name** as it appears on your signage, invoices and website
- **Address** (if customers visit you) or the list of cities you serve (if you travel to them)
- **A local phone number** that your team or an AI receptionist actually answers
- **Website URL**, ideally with a page for each main service
- **Opening hours**, including holiday exceptions
- **10 or more real photos**: storefront, interior, team, vehicles, finished work
- **A Google account** that the business owns, not an employee's personal Gmail

That last point matters. If the employee who created the profile leaves, you can lose control of it. Use a shared business email and add owners and managers through the profile's access settings.

## Check whether a profile already exists

Many California businesses already have a profile they never created. Google builds listings from public data, and previous tenants or old owners may have left one behind.

Search your business name and address on Google Maps. If a listing appears with a "Claim this business" or "Own this business?" option, claim it rather than creating a duplicate. Duplicates split your reviews and can trigger suspension.

If someone else already claimed it, you can request ownership. Google then runs a process that can take days, so start early.

## Step-by-step setup

1. **Go to Google Business Profile** and sign in with the business account.
2. **Enter your exact business name.** No extra keywords, no city name unless it is part of your real name.
3. **Choose your primary category.** This is the most important single choice (see below).
4. **Add your address or service area.** Pick the right model for your business, covered in the next section.
5. **Add phone and website.**
6. **Verify.** Follow the method Google offers.
7. **Complete everything else**: hours, services, description, photos, attributes, booking link.

Do not stop after step 6. A verified but empty profile ranks poorly and converts worse than a full one.

## Storefront or service-area business: pick the right model

This decision causes more California suspensions than any other.

**Storefront or hybrid:** You have a location where customers can meet you during stated hours: a dental office, salon, restaurant, retail shop or showroom. Your address displays publicly.

**Service-area business:** You travel to customers, as an HVAC technician, plumber, mobile detailer or cleaner would. You should hide your address and list the areas you serve instead.

Two rules to follow:

- **Do not use a home address publicly** if customers do not visit you. Hide it and use service areas.
- **Do not use a virtual office, mailbox or co-working desk** as a storefront. Google treats these as fake locations, and they are a common reason for suspension.

If you serve several California cities, list the ones you genuinely cover. Listing 40 cities you have never worked in does not help you rank. It just looks like spam, and a plumber in Fresno does not become relevant in San Diego by naming it.

## Verification: what to expect

Google decides which verification methods you see. Commonly offered options are phone, text, email, postcard and video.

**Video verification** is increasingly the default for new or higher-risk profiles. Local Falcon's guide says the video must be unedited, continuous, at least 30 seconds long, and recorded on a mobile device while logged into the Google account managing the profile.

For a storefront, you will typically need to show:

- Your street and the outside of the building, with signage
- The inside of the premises
- Proof you are connected to the business, such as equipment, stock or a work area

Service-area businesses are generally asked to show their vehicle, tools, branded materials and proof of where the business operates. Record it in one take, in daylight if possible, and do not pause or cut.

If a postcard is offered, it goes to the address you entered, so make sure someone is there to collect the mail. Do not edit the business name or address while verification is pending, because that can restart the process.

## Fill in the profile fields that drive calls

### Categories

Your **primary category** tells Google what you are. Choose the most specific one that fits, such as "Emergency plumber" rather than just "Contractor," if it is accurate. Add secondary categories only for services you genuinely offer.

### Business name

Use your real name. Stuffing a city or service into it ("Best Cheap Plumber Los Angeles") is against Google's guidelines and is a frequent trigger for suspension or for competitors to report you.

### Services and description

List every service you offer, with a short plain description and, where it makes sense, a price or a starting-from range. Write the business description for a customer, not a search engine: what you do, who you serve, what area, and what makes you different.

### Photos

Add real photos of your team, location, vehicles and finished jobs. Stock images add nothing. Refresh them regularly, since a profile with recent photos looks active.

### Hours

Keep them accurate, including holidays. Wrong hours lead to bad reviews from people who show up to a closed door.

### Phone number

Use a local California area code where you can. More importantly, make sure it is answered. A profile that sends calls to voicemail wastes the traffic you worked to earn. If your front desk cannot pick up every call, a missed-call text back or an AI receptionist can catch the overflow.

## California-specific points

**Business registration.** If you operate under a name that is not your own legal name, California generally requires a fictitious business name (DBA) filing, normally made with your county clerk. Google does not ask for this at setup, but a mismatch between your registered name, your signage and your profile creates trust problems with banks, lenders and sometimes Google reviewers. Check your county clerk's requirements for the exact process and timing.

**Licensing.** Many California trades must show a state licence, such as contractors with a CSLB licence number. Put it on your website and in your profile description. It builds trust, and customers in these categories often check.

**Multiple locations.** If you have branches in, say, Sacramento and San Jose, each needs its own profile with its own address, phone number and local page on your website. Do not create one profile and list all the cities.

**Large, spread-out service areas.** California is big. Be realistic about where your crew can reach in a day. A narrower, honest service area tends to produce better leads than a vague statewide one.

## Reviews: the part you cannot fake

Reviews affect both ranking and whether a searcher picks you over the next result. You cannot buy them, and you should not offer incentives for them. Google's policies prohibit fake and incentivised reviews, and getting caught can lead to review removal or profile penalties.

What works:

- **Ask every satisfied customer** straight after the job, while they are happy
- **Send the direct review link** by text or email, so it takes one tap
- **Train your staff** to ask, and to do it the same way each time
- **Reply to every review**, positive or negative, in a calm and professional way

For negative reviews, respond once, factually, and offer to resolve it offline. Do not argue. Prospective customers read your reply more than the complaint.

## Mistakes that get profiles suspended

- Using a virtual office or mailbox as a storefront address
- Adding keywords or city names to the business name
- Creating duplicate profiles for the same location
- Showing a home address when you only travel to customers
- Changing the name, address or category repeatedly after verification
- Buying reviews or asking staff to review the business

A suspension can take your listing off Google until you appeal, and appeals take time. Getting it right the first time is cheaper than fixing it.

## When to handle it yourself and when to get help

Doing it yourself is the right call for a single-location business with a simple setup. It is free, and the steps above cover it.

Paid help makes sense in a few cases:

- You have multiple locations or a large service area
- Your profile was suspended or you have duplicate listings
- You are in a crowded market, such as dentists in Los Angeles, and need a plan beyond the basics
- You do not have the time to post, respond to reviews and track calls

Be cautious of any provider who promises a guaranteed number-one position. No one can guarantee map pack rankings, and results depend on competition, location, reviews and your website. Ask what they will do monthly and how they will report calls and leads.

## FAQ

### Is a Google Business Profile free in California?

Yes. Creating and managing a profile costs nothing, anywhere in the US. You only pay if you hire someone to set it up or manage it.

### How long does it take to verify a Google Business Profile?

It depends on the method Google offers. Phone, text and email are usually quick. Postcards take longer, and video verification can be reviewed at Google's pace. If you are stuck, check that your name and address match your signage and website, and do not make edits while a review is pending.

### Can I list my home as my business address in California?

You can use a home address to verify the profile if you run a legitimate business from it, but you should hide it if customers do not visit you. List your service areas instead. Showing a home address publicly when you only travel to customers goes against Google's guidelines.
