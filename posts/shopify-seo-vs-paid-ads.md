## The short answer: it depends on your cash and your margins

For most Shopify stores, **paid ads should come first if you need sales in the next 30 to 90 days and your margins can absorb ad costs**. **SEO should come first if you have a longer runway, a wide catalog, and thin margins that make paid traffic hard to profit from.** Most stores that last end up doing both, in sequence.

Paid ads rent traffic. You pay per click, and when you stop paying, the traffic stops. SEO builds traffic you do not pay for per visit, but it takes months and costs staff or agency time up front.

The right first dollar depends on three things: how soon you need revenue, what a customer is worth to you, and how many products you sell.

## What each one costs

**Paid ads** have no fixed price. You choose a daily budget on Google or Meta, and the cost per click is set by competition in your category. A starter budget of $1,500 to $3,000 per month is common for a small store, plus management fees if you hire help. Our guide to [Google Ads management costs](https://www.zapplon.com/services) is not a substitute for quotes, so get at least two before you commit.

**Shopify SEO** is mostly labor. One agency, SearchBloom, publishes ranges of roughly $2,000 to $4,000 per month for smaller stores in less competitive categories, $4,000 to $9,000 for established stores with larger catalogs, and $9,000 or more for high-volume or Shopify Plus brands. The same source warns to be cautious of ecommerce SEO priced below roughly $1,000 a month.

Those are one agency's figures, and agencies price differently. Treat them as a sense of scale, not a quote. You can also do basic SEO yourself for the price of your time and a few tools, which is a real option if you have more hours than dollars.

## How fast each one works

Paid ads can produce clicks within a day of launch. Real results take longer, because campaigns need a few weeks of data to settle and you will test several ads and audiences before you find what works. Plan on 4 to 8 weeks before you can judge profitability fairly.

SEO is slower. Ahrefs, an SEO software company, reports that it typically takes three to six months for SEO to show results, based on a poll of 3,680 people. Its separate research on page age found that most top-ranking pages take one to two years to reach the top, though realistic early results often show within 3 to 6 months.

That timeline stretches if your site is new. Ahrefs notes that Google's John Mueller has said it could take up to a year for Google to work out where to rank new sites.

If your store opened last month and you need cash flow this quarter, SEO alone will not get you there.

## Conversion and return: what the benchmarks say

Conversion rates matter more than traffic. If 100 visitors produce 1 order or 3, your costs per sale differ by a factor of three.

Published averages for Shopify stores sit in a narrow band. One benchmark roundup from Enrich Labs reports an average conversion rate of 1.4% to 1.8% across all ecommerce niches. A separate roundup of WordStream data puts the average ecommerce conversion rate for Google Search ads at 2.81%. Treat both as rough guides, because they come from secondary sources and vary by product type and price.

Here is a worked example with made-up numbers, so you can run your own. Say your average order is $80 and your gross margin is 40%, which is $32 of profit per order. If a click costs $1.00 and 2% of visitors buy, each order costs you $50 in ad spend. You lose $18 on every first sale.

That store only makes money on paid traffic if customers reorder, if the order value goes up, or if the click is cheaper. This is why **margin and repeat purchase rate decide whether paid ads work for you**, not the platform.

## When paid ads should come first

Paid ads are the better first spend in these situations:

- **You are launching or relaunching.** A new store has no search authority. Ads give you traffic and sales data while SEO is still building.
- **You sell a single hero product or a small range.** There is not much for SEO to rank, and ads can target buyers directly.
- **You need cash in 90 days.** Inventory, seasonal peaks or a loan repayment do not wait for rankings.
- **Your margins are 50% or higher, or customers reorder.** You can afford a higher cost to acquire the first order.
- **You are testing demand.** Ads tell you within weeks whether a product sells, before you invest in content around it.

## When SEO should come first

SEO is the better first spend in these situations:

- **You have a large catalog.** Hundreds of product and collection pages give search engines many chances to match a shopper's query.
- **Your products are searched by name or need.** People search for things like "linen duvet cover queen" or "gluten-free dog treats" when they are ready to buy.
- **Your margins are thin.** Free clicks from search lift profit in a way paid clicks cannot.
- **You have 6 to 12 months of runway.** You can wait for the payoff.
- **Your category has expensive clicks.** If competitors bid up the cost of every click, organic traffic is the cheaper long-term route.

Organic search can also matter beyond traffic volume. One roundup from Opensend, a vendor, reports organic search at 26.7% of traffic but 31% of revenue for ecommerce, though the same source shows paid search at 36% of revenue from 23% of traffic. The takeaway is that both channels earn their place, not that one wins.

## The mistakes that waste money on both sides

**On paid ads:**

- Sending traffic to a slow or confusing store. Fix product pages and checkout first.
- Judging results after one week.
- Not tracking sales properly, so you cannot tell which ad produced an order.
- Pushing budget up before you know your cost per order.

**On SEO:**

- Hiring on promises of page-one rankings in 30 days. Nobody can guarantee that.
- Paying for blog content with no link to products you sell.
- Ignoring the basics on Shopify: unique product titles, descriptions that are not copied from the supplier, clean collection pages, fast images, and no duplicate pages.
- Expecting results without patience. Many stores quit at month four, just before the payoff starts.

## A simple way to decide and sequence both

Work through these steps:

1. **Calculate profit per order.** Take average order value, subtract product cost, shipping, fees and returns.
2. **Estimate how much you can spend to win one customer.** If a first order leaves you $18 short but repeat buyers make that back, you have a number to work with.
3. **Pick your runway.** Under 90 days of patience points to ads. Over 6 months points to SEO alongside ads.
4. **Fix the store first.** Both channels perform badly on a slow, unclear site.
5. **Set a split.** A common approach is 70 to 80% of budget on ads and 20 to 30% on SEO for a new store, shifting toward SEO as organic traffic grows. That ratio is a rule of thumb, not a verified benchmark, so adjust it to your numbers.
6. **Review quarterly.** Ads are for testing and quick sales. Use what they teach you, such as which products and phrases convert, to pick SEO targets.

That last step is where the two work together. A product that sells well on ads is a strong candidate for a page you want to rank for.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is SEO cheaper than paid ads for a Shopify store?

Over a long period, usually yes, per visit. In the first six months, usually no. You pay for SEO work before traffic arrives, while ads cost you per click from day one. Based on the agency range above, a professional SEO retainer starts around $2,000 per month for a smaller store, which is comparable to a modest ad budget.

### How long before Shopify SEO brings in sales?

Plan for 3 to 6 months before you see meaningful change, and up to 12 months in competitive categories or on a new domain, according to the Ahrefs figures above. Low-competition, specific product searches can move sooner.

### Can I do Shopify SEO myself?

Yes, for the basics. Write original product descriptions, use clear page titles, compress images, and build collection pages around phrases buyers use. Hire help when you have a large catalog, technical problems, or no time to keep at it.

## What to do next

If you need sales soon and your numbers support it, start with a small, tracked ad budget and fix your product pages at the same time. If you have a wide catalog and time, start with SEO and keep a small ad budget to learn what sells.

Either way, write down your profit per order before you spend anything. It is the one figure that tells you which channel you can afford.
