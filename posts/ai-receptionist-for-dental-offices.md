## What an AI receptionist for a dental office costs

For a single-location practice, an AI receptionist typically costs **$200 to $800 a month**. Vendor guides from Viva AI and Keragon, and an Oral Health Group feature, all give that range. Cheaper tools start around $79 to $100 a month. Full platforms bundled with patient messaging and scheduling can run higher.

Setup is often free or a modest one-off fee, but this varies by vendor, so ask. Compare the monthly figure with the alternative. Glassdoor puts the average US dental front desk salary at about $39,500 a year, and about $45,000 in Los Angeles. That is before payroll tax, benefits and cover for lunch, sick days and holidays.

An AI receptionist does not replace the person at your front desk. It answers the calls that person cannot get to. The rest of this guide explains what that means for your budget, your schedule and your patients.

## What an AI receptionist does on a dental phone line

An AI receptionist is software that answers your phone in a natural-sounding voice, talks to the caller and takes action. It is not the old "press 1 for appointments" menu. Callers speak normally and it responds.

In a dental office, the usual jobs are:

- Answering instantly, including after hours, at lunch and when both front desk staff are with patients
- Booking, rescheduling and cancelling appointments in your calendar
- Answering routine questions: opening hours, location, parking, accepted insurance, new patient process
- Capturing details from new patients and sending them to your team
- Recognising emergency wording, such as a broken tooth or facial swelling, and passing the call to a person or on-call dentist
- Sending text confirmations or intake links after the call

## Why dental offices look at this: missed calls

The main reason practices buy is missed calls. Several vendor sources, including Viva AI and Aria Dental, put the share of unanswered calls at roughly 28% to 38% during business hours. One source attributes a 38% figure for new patient calls to the American Dental Association, but we could not verify that on an ADA page, so treat it as indicative.

These numbers come from companies selling the fix, so check your own. Most phone systems and call tracking tools show missed and abandoned calls. A week of data is enough to tell you whether you have a problem.

Then work out what a call is worth. Viva AI uses roughly $200 per new patient call as an industry figure, once the first visit and later treatment are counted. Your number will differ. A practice that does implants or Invisalign has a higher value per patient than one doing mostly hygiene visits.

A simple test: if you miss 15 calls a week, and five are new patients, and your first-year value per new patient is $800, that is real money. Even if the AI books only half of them, it likely pays for itself within a month.

## How much setup involves, step by step

Setup is lighter than most owners expect, but it is not plug and play. Plan on **one to three weeks** from signing to going live. That timeline varies by vendor and by how complicated your practice management software is.

1. **Sign the paperwork first.** This includes a Business Associate Agreement (see the HIPAA section below).
2. **Connect your calendar.** Good vendors integrate with the software you already use, such as Open Dental, Dentrix, Eaglesoft or Denticon. Weave, Arini and NexHealth list these integrations. Check yours specifically.
3. **Tell it what you do.** You supply hours, services, insurance plans accepted, new patient rules, appointment lengths per provider and the questions you hear every day.
4. **Define emergencies and handoffs.** Decide what counts as an emergency, who gets the call and what happens if that person does not answer.
5. **Forward or port your number.** Most offices forward calls to the AI after a set number of rings, or after hours only. Weave-style platforms replace the phone system entirely.
6. **Test with real calls.** Call it yourself, and have staff try awkward scenarios: a caller who rambles, a heavy accent, a request to see a specific hygienist.
7. **Start narrow.** Many practices begin with after-hours and overflow, then expand to daytime once they trust it.

## What it costs beyond the monthly fee

The headline price is rarely the full price. Ask about these before you sign:

- **Minute or call limits.** Some plans include a fixed number of minutes, for example CloudTalk lists $99 a month for 200 minutes. At two or three minutes a call, a busy practice can use that up quickly.
- **Per-location pricing.** Multi-office groups are usually quoted by volume.
- **Platform bundles.** Weave starts at about $199 a month for its core platform, with AI receptionist pricing quoted separately, according to Keragon and CloudTalk. You may be buying a phone system, not just an AI.
- **Integration fees.** Some vendors charge for connecting to older practice management software.
- **Contract length.** Annual terms are common. Ask for a monthly or 30-day trial first.

Prices in this market change often and many vendors only publish a quote, so treat any figure in a blog post, including this one, as a starting point.

## HIPAA: the paperwork that matters most

A caller saying "I have a throbbing molar" is sharing health information. Dentists are HIPAA covered entities, and a vendor that handles those calls for you is a business associate. That means you need a **signed Business Associate Agreement (BAA)** before any real patient call goes through the system.

Credminds reported in an August 2026 review that only about half of leading dental AI vendors documented a BAA on their own site. A vendor saying it is "HIPAA compliant" is not the same as signing one.

Before you buy, ask for:

- A signed BAA, in writing
- Confirmation that call recordings and transcripts are encrypted and where they are stored
- A list of any subcontractors, such as the AI model provider, that touch the data
- Their breach notification process
- How long recordings are kept and how you can delete them

Also check your state rules on recording calls. California requires all-party consent for recording, so the AI should announce that the call may be recorded or that the caller is speaking to an automated assistant. Ask your own attorney or compliance adviser to confirm what applies to you.

## Limits and when it is a poor fit

An AI receptionist is good at repetitive, predictable calls. It is weaker at the ones that matter most for relationships.

**Where it struggles:**

- Anxious, upset or elderly patients who want a person
- Insurance questions that need a benefits check or judgement
- Complex treatment questions, billing disputes and anything clinical
- Noisy lines, strong accents and unusual names, which can cause transcription errors
- Calls with two people, such as a parent booking for a child with a different surname

**When you probably should not buy:**

- You already answer nearly every call, and your missed call rate is under 5%
- You get very few calls, say under 10 a day, where a $50 to $100 a month answering service or voicemail with text-back may be enough
- Your practice management software has no integration, so staff have to re-enter every booking by hand
- Your front desk is overloaded by in-person tasks, in which case a part-time hire may help more

The honest middle ground for many practices is a hybrid. The AI handles overflow, after hours and simple bookings. People handle patients standing in front of them and anything sensitive.

## How to choose and test a vendor

Run a two-week pilot before you commit. Pick two or three vendors and ask each for a demo using your real hours, services and insurance list, not a generic script.

Score each on these points:

- **Booking accuracy.** Did it book the right appointment type, with the right provider, at a time that is actually free?
- **Emergency handling.** It should hand off on emergency language you define. It should not decide urgency alone.
- **Handoff quality.** When it transfers, does your staff get a summary, or does the patient repeat everything?
- **Transcript review.** Read at least 20 real call transcripts. This is the fastest way to find mistakes.
- **Support.** Who fixes it when it books a cleaning into a root canal slot?

Track three numbers before and after: **answer rate** (aim for 95% or higher), **new patient bookings per week**, and **front desk interruptions** per day. If those do not move after a month, cancel.

## FAQ

### How much does an AI receptionist cost for a dental office?

Most single-location practices pay **$200 to $800 a month**, depending on call volume, integrations and features. Entry tools start around $79 to $100 a month, and bundled platforms such as Weave cost more. Always ask about minute limits, setup fees and contract length.

### Is an AI receptionist HIPAA compliant for dentists?

It can be, but only if the vendor signs a Business Associate Agreement with you and protects patient data properly. Get the BAA in writing before any patient calls are routed to it. A "HIPAA compliant" claim on a website is not enough.

### Will an AI receptionist replace my front desk staff?

For most practices, no. It takes overflow, after-hours and routine booking calls so your team can focus on patients in the office. Practices that cut staff entirely tend to struggle with complex calls, anxious patients and insurance questions.

## What to do next

Start with a week of missed call data and a rough value per new patient. If the numbers show a real gap, shortlist two vendors that integrate with your practice software and will sign a BAA. Run a short pilot on after-hours and overflow calls first, then decide whether to expand to the main line.
