## The short answer: what a marketing agency costs in California

Most small and mid-sized businesses in California pay **$1,000 to $12,000 per month** for a marketing agency on a retainer. Small businesses usually sit at the lower end of that range. Project work, such as a website or a campaign launch, typically runs **$5,000 to $50,000 or more**.

Those figures come from US-wide pricing guides, including WebFX's 2026 agency pricing guide. We could not find a reliable published dataset that isolates California agency prices. Expect to pay at or above the national figures in Los Angeles, San Francisco and San Diego, because agency salaries and rents are higher there. Agencies in the Central Valley or Inland Empire, or remote agencies, can price lower.

Your ad budget is a separate cost. The agency fee pays for the people doing the work. The money you spend on Google, Meta or Instagram ads goes to those platforms.

## How agencies charge: four pricing models

How an agency charges affects what you pay as much as the services do. You will meet four models.

- **Monthly retainer.** A fixed fee for an agreed scope of work. This is the most common model. Pricing guides put typical US retainers at $1,000 to $12,000 a month, with some larger programs far higher.
- **Percentage of ad spend.** The agency takes a cut of your ad budget. Guides on Google Ads management commonly cite 10% to 20%, often with a minimum fee of around $500 a month. On a $5,000 ad budget at 15%, you would pay $750.
- **Hourly.** Rates vary widely by source and agency size. Clutch data cited by Red Shoes Inc. puts most US agencies at $100 to $149 an hour. Other guides report anything from $50 to $400.
- **Project fee.** A one-off price for a defined deliverable, such as a new website, a Shopify store rebuild or an ad campaign setup.

Neither a flat retainer nor a percentage is always better. A percentage can look cheap on a small budget, but it grows as you spend more. A flat fee is easier to plan around, but can feel expensive in a slow month.

## What you pay by service

Prices depend on what you ask the agency to do. These are typical US ranges from published 2026 guides.

- **Local SEO:** WebFX and SEO.com both put local SEO at about $500 to $3,000 a month. For a single-location business in a mid-sized market, Savo Group reports $1,000 to $2,000 a month is typical.
- **Google Ads management:** Commonly 10% to 20% of ad spend, or a flat fee of roughly $500 to $5,000 a month depending on account size.
- **Social media management:** WebFX-based figures put this at about $500 to $5,000 a month.
- **Full-service digital marketing:** Several guides, including Red Shoes Inc. and mthd.agency, place ongoing agency work between $2,500 and $15,000 a month.

Ranges are wide because scope varies. A $700 local SEO package might update your Google Business Profile and fix a few listings. A $3,000 package might include new pages, content, review generation and monthly reporting.

## What your own budget should look like

A useful way to plan is to work backwards from what a customer is worth to you. If a new client brings in $2,000 in profit and you close one in five leads, you can afford to pay a good deal per lead.

As a rough starting point, many small businesses begin with **$1,000 to $3,000 a month** for a focused engagement on one channel, as Red Shoes Inc. notes, and add more once results are clear. Add your ad spend on top.

Here are three realistic examples for a California business:

- **Single-location dental or chiropractic office:** Local SEO at $1,000 to $2,000 a month, plus $1,500 to $3,000 a month in Google Ads and a management fee of $500 or more.
- **HVAC or plumbing company:** Google Ads with a percentage-based fee or flat fee, plus local SEO. Total agency fees of $1,500 to $4,000 a month are common, with ad spend depending on how many service calls you can handle.
- **Shopify store:** A mix of Meta ads and Google Shopping, with agency fees usually $2,000 to $5,000 a month and ad spend proportional to your margins.

These are planning examples built from the ranges above, not quotes. Your market and competition will move the numbers.

## What drives the price up or down

Several factors explain why two quotes for the same service can differ by thousands of dollars.

- **Location and competition.** Ranking for "personal injury lawyer Los Angeles" is far more expensive than ranking for "plumber Fresno". Competitive markets need more work and bigger ad budgets.
- **Agency size.** Swydo's pricing data shows small agencies of 2 to 10 people typically charge a monthly minimum of $1,500 to $5,000, while freelancers start lower at $500 to $2,000. You get less overhead from a freelancer, but also less coverage if they are ill or busy.
- **Number of locations.** Each extra location needs its own listings, pages and reviews.
- **Scope and deliverables.** Creative work such as video and photography costs more than reporting and optimisation.
- **Contract length.** Mthd.agency notes that many agencies ask for a minimum of three to six months.

## Costs that rarely appear in the first quote

Ask about these before you sign, because they are where budgets drift.

- **Setup or onboarding fees.** Some agencies charge a one-off fee for audits, tracking and account setup.
- **Ad creative.** Photos, video and landing pages are sometimes extra.
- **Software and call tracking.** Call tracking, CRM and reporting tools may be billed to you.
- **Ad spend minimums.** An agency may say it needs a certain budget before it takes you on, because below a threshold it cannot make the campaign work.
- **Cancellation terms.** Check the notice period and who owns the ad accounts, websites and Google Business Profile when you leave.

Insist that your ad accounts are in your name. If an agency owns them, you could lose your campaign history when you part ways.

## When an agency is not the right choice

An agency is not always the right buy, and a cheaper option is sometimes better.

- **Your budget is under about $1,000 a month in total.** At that level, agency fees take most of the money. A freelancer or doing the basics yourself will go further. Start by claiming and fully completing your Google Business Profile, asking happy customers for reviews, and fixing your website's phone number and opening hours.
- **You cannot handle more leads.** If your front desk already misses calls, more marketing wastes money. Fix call handling first. An answering service or AI receptionist is often cheaper than extra ads.
- **You want guaranteed results.** Be wary of any agency that promises a ranking or a number of leads. SEO and ads depend on competition, your reviews, your website and your offer.
- **You have no way of tracking enquiries.** If you cannot tell which calls and forms came from marketing, you cannot judge the agency. Set up call tracking first.

## How to choose and compare quotes

Get three quotes and compare them on the same terms. Ask each agency the same questions.

1. What exactly is included each month, and how many hours does that represent?
2. Who will do the work, and who will I speak to?
3. How will you measure results, and how often will I see a report?
4. Do I own the ad accounts, website and data?
5. What is the minimum term, and how do I cancel?
6. Can I speak to a client in a similar business in California?

Judge results by **cost per lead** and **cost per customer**, not by clicks or impressions. A $2,000 monthly package that produces 20 qualified calls is cheaper than a $700 package that produces two.

## FAQ

### How much should a small business in California spend on marketing?

There is no single correct figure. Many small businesses start at $1,000 to $3,000 a month in agency fees for one channel, plus ad spend. A practical test is to work out what a new customer is worth, then set a budget your sales team can convert profitably.

### Are California marketing agencies more expensive than elsewhere?

Often, in the large metro areas, because staff and office costs are higher. We did not find a verified California-only price survey, so treat national figures as a baseline and expect Los Angeles and Bay Area agencies to quote higher than agencies in smaller markets or remote teams.

### Is a monthly retainer or a percentage of ad spend better?

A flat retainer is easier to budget. A percentage of ad spend, typically 10% to 20%, can suit growing accounts but means your fee rises as you spend more. Whichever you choose, ask what work you get for the fee, not just the rate.

## What to do next

Decide your total monthly budget, including ad spend, and the one result you want most, such as booked appointments or phone calls. Then request three written quotes covering the same scope. Choose the agency that is clear about what it does, lets you own your accounts and reports on cost per lead.
