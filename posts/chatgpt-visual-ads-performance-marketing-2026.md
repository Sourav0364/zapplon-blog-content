## What OpenAI announced about ChatGPT visual ads

On October 5, OpenAI announced a **new visual ad format in ChatGPT**. The company says the initial test will place ads during image generation, where the creative can show product inspiration, product use, or an experience a service makes possible. OpenAI said the ads will be clearly labeled, remain separate from the image being created, and will not influence ChatGPT’s answers. Testing is scheduled to begin later this month in the United States with an initial group of advertisers.

That makes **ChatGPT visual ads** a timely topic for performance marketers—but it is important to distinguish an announcement from a widely available placement. As of the announcement, OpenAI described a planned test, not an open inventory guarantee for every brand, a published creative specification, or proof of a particular campaign result. Brands should prepare a learning plan rather than assume the format will behave like an established social feed.

This guide turns the news into a practical preparation checklist: what visual assets to review, how to define a test, what to measure, and what to verify as the platform publishes more details.

## Understand the placement before designing for it

The creative context matters. OpenAI says the initial format will appear **during image generation** and will remain separate from the image a user asks ChatGPT to create. That is different from an ad embedded inside a generated image, and the company says its advertising does not affect the answers ChatGPT provides.

Those statements give marketers some early boundaries, but leave important execution details open. The announcement does not provide a complete public specification for dimensions, file types, campaign buying, targeting, reporting windows, or advertiser eligibility. Avoid building a launch plan around guesses. Before booking or uploading creative, confirm the current requirements in OpenAI’s advertising materials and with the account team or platform interface available to your business.

A good first step is a **format-readiness audit**. Gather the questions your team needs answered:

- What creative formats, sizes, file limits, and landing-page rules will apply?
- Which audiences, locations, and campaign objectives can participate in the test?
- What reporting and attribution options will be available to each advertiser?
- How will the ad be labeled and separated from generated imagery in the user experience?
- What review process applies to sensitive categories, claims, and product imagery?

This is not a reason to delay all preparation. It is a way to separate the work you can do now—asset quality, landing pages, measurement design—from details that should wait for official specifications.

## Build product imagery around a useful story

OpenAI describes the format as a way for people to explore products and services through images showing inspiration, use, or the experiences an offering makes possible. Marketers can use that direction to assess whether their image library explains the product clearly, rather than simply decorating an ad.

For each candidate image, ask:

- **Can a new customer identify the product or service quickly?** Keep the main subject prominent and avoid visual clutter.
- **Does the scene show a credible use case?** A contextual image should reflect how the offering is actually used, not imply a capability or result the business cannot substantiate.
- **Does the destination continue the same story?** Match product names, pricing, availability, and promises between the visual, ad text, and landing page.
- **Are the assets rights-cleared?** Confirm that the business can use the photography, illustrations, logos, and people shown in paid promotion.
- **Can the creative work without unsupported claims?** Review health, financial, performance, and comparative statements against evidence and applicable advertising rules.

Prepare a small set of distinct creative concepts, each tied to a customer question or use case. For example, a retailer might test imagery focused on product detail against imagery showing the product in a real-life setting—if the final format supports both approaches. Keep a record of what each image is intended to communicate so the team can learn from results instead of treating every asset as interchangeable.

## Define a measurement plan before spending

A new placement is only useful to a performance team if the test has a clear question and a credible way to evaluate it. Before a campaign begins, agree on the business outcome that matters: qualified site visits, product views, leads, purchases, or another action aligned with the objective. Avoid relying on a single top-line metric if it does not connect to the business goal.

OpenAI says it is expanding its measurement tools and partner ecosystem. Its October 5 announcement describes integrations with Hightouch, Tealium, and LiveRamp for sending conversion data from existing systems, and names several attribution and measurement partners. Availability and suitability can vary by setup, so confirm the options that apply to your account rather than assuming every integration or measurement method is enabled for every advertiser.

For your own test plan, document:

- The hypothesis the campaign is testing.
- The audience, offer, landing page, and creative variants in scope.
- The primary conversion and any supporting indicators.
- The data source for each metric and the attribution method used.
- The comparison period or baseline and any other campaigns running at the same time.
- The person responsible for reviewing results and deciding whether to continue.

If the format is new and the sample is small, treat early results as directional. Record what the data can and cannot establish. Do not claim that an ad caused a sale solely because the sale happened after someone saw the ad; use the measurement method available and state its limitations.

## Include brand suitability and privacy in launch readiness

OpenAI says it is developing brand-suitability evaluation pilots with DoubleVerify and Integral Ad Science. The announcement describes these as efforts to help advertisers understand the environment and how safeguards are applied; it does not say that every advertiser already has a finished third-party verification option. Ask what placement controls, reporting, and independent review are actually available before making a suitability commitment to stakeholders.

Conversation-based platforms also raise questions that teams may not face in exactly the same way on a conventional web page. Establish internal boundaries before a test:

- Decide which products, claims, or contexts are unsuitable for the brand.
- Confirm how ad placement controls work and what exclusions can be requested.
- Ensure landing pages and conversion collection follow the company’s privacy notices and consent practices.
- Limit data sent for measurement to what the approved setup requires.
- Escalate policy questions about sensitive products or regulated claims before launch.

Treat platform statements and verification pilots as inputs to due diligence—not substitutes for the brand’s own legal, privacy, and policy review.

## A practical ChatGPT visual ads launch checklist

Use this checklist to get ready without assuming features that have not been announced:

1. **Confirm access and availability.** Verify whether your business is eligible for the US test and what buying process applies.
2. **Read the current specifications.** Check permitted assets, text, landing pages, labeling, and campaign controls from official sources.
3. **Review creative rights and accuracy.** Make sure every product image and claim is approved for paid use.
4. **Prepare a focused test.** Choose a clear audience, offer, objective, and small set of meaningful creative differences.
5. **Validate measurement.** Confirm conversion events, partner integrations, attribution windows, and reporting access before launch.
6. **Set brand and privacy guardrails.** Document unacceptable contexts, data-handling boundaries, and who approves exceptions.
7. **Write down the learning agenda.** Decide what evidence would justify changing creative, expanding a test, or stopping it.

The format may evolve as OpenAI learns from its initial test. Keep the plan modular: separate reusable product imagery and measurement setup from assumptions about a specific placement. Recheck the official guidance before committing substantial budget.
