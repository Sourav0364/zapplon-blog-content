## The short answer: clicks are not the problem

Google Ads clicks with no conversions usually come from one of four places: the wrong search terms, a landing page that does not make calling easy, a phone number or form that is not working or not being tracked, or calls that arrive and go unanswered. Each is fixable, and most can be checked in a single afternoon.

Clicks only prove that your ad was shown to someone and looked relevant. They do not prove the person wanted to buy. If you are paying for clicks and getting no calls, you are paying Google to send people who look at your site and leave.

Start by working out which of the four problems you have. The rest of this post walks through them in the order that saves the most money.

## What a normal result looks like

Before you decide your ads are broken, check what is normal. WordStream publishes Google Ads benchmarks by industry. In its table, the average search click-through rate for consumer services is 2.41%, and the average search conversion rate for consumer services is 6.64%. Home goods sits lower, at 2.70%, and health and medical at 3.36% (figures also reproduced by Store Growers). These are averages across many accounts, so treat them as a rough guide only.

In plain terms, a typical service business should expect somewhere between 3 and 7 conversions from every 100 clicks. If you have had 200 clicks and zero calls or forms, that is well outside normal and something specific is wrong.

If you have had only 30 or 40 clicks, the sample may simply be too small. Wait until you have at least 100 clicks before judging.

## Check your tracking before anything else

The cheapest explanation is that calls are happening and you cannot see them. Check this first, because it changes every decision after it.

Common tracking gaps for local businesses:

- **Call tracking is not set up.** If the only conversion action is a form fill, every phone call from the ad is invisible.
- **Calls from the ad itself are counted, but calls from your website are not.** Someone who clicks through and then dials the number on your page needs a separate tracking setup.
- **The conversion is firing on the wrong thing.** A conversion set to "page view" will look great and mean nothing. One set to nothing will show zero.
- **The form works on your computer but not on phones.** Most local searches happen on mobile, so test it on your own phone.

Do a real test. Click your own ad from your phone, call the number, submit the form, and confirm each one appears in Google Ads within a day. Use a different device or network, and avoid clicking your own ads repeatedly, since that wastes budget.

## Look at the search terms you are paying for

This is the most common cause of expensive clicks with no result. In Google Ads, open the **search terms report** and read the actual phrases people typed before clicking. You will often find searches that have nothing to do with buying.

Typical examples:

- A plumber paying for "plumber jobs" or "how to become a plumber"
- A dentist paying for "dental school" or "free dental clinic near me"
- A roofer paying for "roofing materials" from DIY shoppers
- Any business paying for "cheap", "free" or "DIY" searches when it sells a premium service

The fix is **negative keywords**. Add the unwanted words so your ads stop showing for them. Review the report weekly for the first month, then monthly.

Also check your **match types**. Broad match lets Google show your ad for loosely related searches. It can work with strong conversion tracking and enough data, but on a small budget it often burns money on irrelevant clicks. Phrase and exact match give you tighter control.

## Check the location and the timing

Local businesses lose money when ads show to people who cannot become customers.

- **Radius too wide.** If you serve Orange County, an ad also shown across Los Angeles and San Diego counties attracts people who will not drive to you. Tighten the area to where you actually work.
- **Location setting.** Make sure it targets people physically in or regularly in your area, not anyone showing "interest" in it. Otherwise travellers and researchers elsewhere click your ad.
- **Ad schedule.** If you run ads at 11pm and nobody answers until 9am, the caller has already phoned your competitor. Either limit ads to staffed hours or make sure calls are covered after hours.

## Make sure the landing page earns the call

If your searches look right, look at where the click lands. Open your landing page on a phone and pretend you have a leaking pipe or a broken tooth and want help now.

A page that converts for service businesses usually has:

- The phone number at the top, tappable with one touch
- A headline that matches the search ("Emergency AC repair in Irvine", not "Welcome to our company")
- One clear action, such as call or request a quote
- Proof that is easy to see: reviews, licence numbers, years in business, photos of real work
- A price signal or "starting from" figure where you can offer it
- A form with 3 or 4 fields, not 10

Sending every ad to your home page is a frequent mistake. A home page tries to serve everyone, so it serves nobody well. One page per service, matched to the ad, almost always beats a general page.

Speed matters too. Run the page through Google's free PageSpeed Insights. If it takes several seconds to load on mobile, many visitors leave before they see the number.

## Check whether the calls are being answered

Sometimes the ads work, the calls arrive, and the business never knows. A call that rings out, goes to a full voicemail, or reaches a rushed person who cannot book it does not turn into a customer. It also looks like a failed campaign in your reports.

Ask yourself:

- What share of calls during business hours do you pick up? Check your phone records, not your memory.
- Do callers get a call back within an hour?
- What happens at lunch, after 5pm and on weekends?

Many callers who reach voicemail do not leave a message and move on to the next result. Our [missed calls post](https://www.zapplon.com/blog) covers the cost in more detail. If you find that a large share of ad calls go unanswered, fix that before raising your ad budget. An answering service, a part-time receptionist or an AI receptionist can all cover the gap. An AI option is not right for everyone, especially if your calls are complex or emotional, so compare it with a human service on your actual call types.

## Fix the offer, the bid and the budget

If tracking, search terms, page and phones are all sound, the problem may be commercial.

**Your offer is weak.** If five competitors show next to you with free estimates, same-day service and strong reviews, "Call us for a quote" will lose. Look at the ads and Google Business Profile listings above and below yours, and see what you are not offering.

**Your reviews are thin.** Local searchers compare star ratings fast. A business with 12 reviews and a 4.1 average will lose calls to one with 180 reviews and a 4.8, even with the same ad.

**Your budget is too small for the keywords.** Costs per click in competitive service categories such as legal, home services and medical can be high, and they vary by city and season. If your daily budget buys only a few clicks, you cannot collect enough data to learn anything. Either narrow to fewer, more specific keywords or raise the budget. **Automated bidding has no data.** Smart bidding strategies need conversion data to work. If tracking is broken, the system is optimising toward nothing and may chase cheap, low-quality clicks.

## A one-week diagnostic checklist

Work through this in order. Stop at the first thing you find broken, fix it, and wait a week before moving on.

1. Place a test call and test form submission from your phone, and confirm both are recorded.
2. Pull the last 30 days of the search terms report and add negative keywords for irrelevant searches.
3. Check your location and schedule settings against your real service area and staffed hours.
4. Open the landing page on your phone and check for a tappable number, a matching headline and a short form.
5. Compare the number of recorded calls with the calls on your phone bill or phone system.
6. Compare your offer and reviews against the three competitors that show above you.
7. Review budget against the number of clicks you are getting per day.

If you complete all seven and still see nothing after another 100 or so clicks, pause the campaign. Spending more on a campaign you have not diagnosed rarely helps.

## When Google Ads may not be the right fit

Google Ads is not right for every business. It tends to struggle when:

- Your service is rarely searched for, so there is little demand to capture
- Your average job value is low compared with the cost per click
- You cannot answer calls or follow up quickly
- Your market is saturated and your offer matches everyone else's

In some of these cases, local SEO, a well-maintained Google Business Profile or Meta ads can bring in leads at lower cost. Which is better depends on whether people actively search for what you sell or need to be prompted to want it.

## FAQ

### How many clicks should I wait for before deciding my Google Ads are not working?

Aim for at least 100 clicks, and ideally a few weeks of data. At a typical service conversion rate of around 3% to 7%, 100 clicks should produce a handful of enquiries. Fewer clicks than that is too small a sample to judge.

### Why do I get clicks but no calls from Google Ads?

The most likely causes are irrelevant search terms, a landing page that makes calling hard, broken or missing call tracking, and calls that go unanswered. Check tracking first, then the search terms report.

### Should I increase my budget if I am not getting conversions?

Not until you have found the cause. More budget on a broken setup means more wasted clicks. Fix tracking, search terms and the landing page first, then raise spend if the numbers are healthy.
