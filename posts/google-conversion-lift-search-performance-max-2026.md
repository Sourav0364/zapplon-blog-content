## Why Google Conversion Lift for Search and Performance Max matters

A campaign can appear in an attribution report when someone converts, but that report does not always answer the harder question: did the ad cause an additional conversion, or did it receive credit for an action that would have happened anyway? That is the difference between attribution and incrementality.

In early October 2026, [Search Engine Land reported](https://searchengineland.com/google-opens-conversion-lift-to-search-and-performance-max-494195) that Google expanded self-serve Conversion Lift based on users to Search and Performance Max campaigns. These campaign types join Display, Video, Demand Gen, and App campaigns in the listed support set. Google’s [Conversion Lift help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12005564) describes the measurement as an estimate of incremental conversions, site visits, or other actions driven by ads. It also documents eligibility requirements, so the feature is not automatically available to every account.

For teams considering **Google Conversion Lift for Search and Performance Max**, the practical opportunity is a more direct test of added impact—not a replacement for conversion tracking, attribution reports, or careful experiment design. First determine whether the account qualifies, then make sure the business question is clear enough to justify a holdout study.

## Attribution and incrementality answer different questions

Attribution organizes credit for conversions across marketing interactions according to a selected model or measurement system. It can help a marketer compare reported outcomes across campaigns and make day-to-day optimization decisions. But credited conversions alone do not necessarily establish what would have happened without the campaign.

An incrementality study uses a comparison: an eligible group of users is exposed to ads, while a holdout group is not exposed to the campaigns being measured. The difference in observed behavior helps estimate conversions associated with exposure that may not otherwise have occurred. Google's documentation describes Conversion Lift as measuring the incremental number of conversions, site visits, and other actions directly driven by ads.

This distinction is useful when a team is evaluating questions such as:

- Are Search campaigns adding conversions beyond existing demand?
- Does a Performance Max campaign contribute additional outcomes alongside other marketing activity?
- Is a reported increase likely to reflect incremental impact or a shift in credited conversions?

A lift result is evidence for a defined study, not a universal verdict on a channel. It should be interpreted alongside the study setup, conversion actions, campaign changes, and other marketing activity during the test.

## What changed for Search and Performance Max

Search Engine Land’s October 7 report says user-based Conversion Lift became available for eligible advertisers to set up for Search and Performance Max campaigns without first needing help from a Google representative. The report says other supported campaign types include Display, Video, Demand Gen, and App campaigns.

The expansion can make it easier for some marketers to evaluate Search and Performance Max with a lift study in Google Ads. It does **not** mean that every advertiser can launch one immediately. Google's documentation says campaign eligibility, account availability, and conversion compatibility matter. Campaigns in alpha or beta may require assistance from a Google representative; the help page also lists campaign-type exceptions and study limits.

Before building a media plan around this option, check the current Google Ads interface and eligibility documentation for the specific account. Feature access can differ, and eligibility information can change over time.

## Check eligibility before planning a study

Google's current help documentation specifies several important requirements for user-based Conversion Lift:

- **Observed conversions:** at least 1,000 observed conversions; conversions using supplementary data do not count toward this minimum.
- **Campaign budget:** the study requires a minimum campaign budget of $5,000 USD.
- **Compatible conversion action:** the account must track at least one conversion action supported by Conversion Lift.
- **Eligible campaign:** the campaign type must be supported, and account availability can vary.
- **Study capacity:** the help page says a campaign can be part of a maximum of one study at a time across Brand Lift, Search Lift, or Conversion Lift.

Treat these as setup checks, not as a prediction that a study will be available or produce a conclusive result. Confirm eligibility in the account before making budget or timing commitments. If the account is not eligible, continue using the measurement methods available to the business rather than treating a lift study as a required reporting standard.

## Define the question and comparison in advance

A useful measurement plan begins with a decision the results could inform. “Measure campaign performance” is too broad. A more actionable question identifies the specific campaigns and outcome under review, such as whether a defined Search campaign adds purchases or qualified leads during a planned period.

Before launching, document:

1. **The decision:** What will the team change if the study suggests incremental impact—or not?
2. **The campaign scope:** Which eligible campaign or campaigns will be included?
3. **The conversion action:** Is it compatible, tracked consistently, and meaningful to the business?
4. **The timing:** What campaign edits, seasonal events, or promotions may affect interpretation?
5. **The comparison:** Which users or activity are included in the test and holdout, as configured in the study?
6. **The owner:** Who will monitor setup and review the result with the wider marketing team?

Keep the test as interpretable as possible. If major budget, targeting, creative, landing-page, or offer changes happen during the measurement period, record them. This context helps reviewers avoid assigning every observed movement to the campaign alone.

## Read the result with the right caveats

A lift study can help answer a causal question more directly than an attribution report, but it is still a measurement with boundaries. Results relate to the campaigns, period, users, and conversion actions in the study. They should not automatically be generalized to every campaign, future period, or marketing channel.

Use the result alongside operational context:

- Confirm the campaign and conversion action match the original question.
- Review material changes during the study period.
- Consider whether the measured outcome represents business value, not only a platform event.
- Compare the lift finding with other evidence, such as sales, lead quality, or first-party records where available.
- Avoid treating a single study as proof that all other media contributed nothing.

If eligibility or setup is unclear, check the account-specific guidance or consult the platform representative. A test that is poorly matched to a business question can consume time and budget without making the next decision clearer.

## A practical checklist for performance marketers

Before you add a lift study to your Google Ads measurement plan, work through this short checklist:

- Is the business question specifically about incremental conversions or another supported action?
- Does the account meet the observed-conversion and minimum-budget requirements in Google’s current documentation?
- Is the chosen conversion action compatible and consistently recorded?
- Are the Search or Performance Max campaigns eligible in this account?
- Can the team keep the test interpretable and document any major changes?
- Is there an agreed next step for each plausible result?

When the answer to these questions is clear, Conversion Lift can be considered as one part of a broader performance marketing measurement system. If not, resolve the measurement foundations first. Better reporting begins with a useful question, reliable conversion data, and a plan for acting on what is learned.

## FAQ: Google Conversion Lift for Search and Performance Max

### What does Google Conversion Lift measure?

Google describes it as measuring incremental conversions, site visits, and other actions directly driven by ads, using a study that compares exposed and holdout users.

### Can every advertiser use Conversion Lift for Search or Performance Max?

No. Google lists account and campaign eligibility requirements, including at least 1,000 observed conversions, a minimum campaign budget of $5,000 USD, and a compatible conversion action. Availability can vary.

### Is Conversion Lift the same as an attribution report?

No. Attribution assigns credit according to a measurement model; Conversion Lift uses a holdout comparison to estimate the incremental impact of the campaigns in a study.

### Does a lift study guarantee a particular campaign result?

No. It estimates impact for the defined study conditions. Results should be interpreted in context and should not be assumed to apply to every campaign or future period.
