## Why AI product videos are a seller issue now

Product videos are becoming a familiar part of online shopping. They can show how an item looks, explain a use case, answer a common question, or give a shopper another reason to explore a listing. Generative AI can make it easier to produce those videos, especially when a seller has many products or needs content for multiple placements.

But the speed of generation creates a new responsibility: **the seller must know what appears beside the product link**. A video can look polished and still misrepresent the product. It might use the wrong colour, change the scale, invent a feature, or describe the item in a tone that does not fit the brand.

This risk is receiving fresh attention after CNA reported on sellers in Singapore who found AI-generated product videos on Shopee and TikTok that they said were inaccurate or created without their approval. One author saw a video that distorted her book’s cover and presented its themes incorrectly. A design-studio owner described videos in which calendars and stickers appeared in the wrong size or colour. The examples are a reminder that ecommerce video automation needs a review system, not just a generation button.

The practical SEO question for sellers is not simply “How do I make more AI videos?” It is “How do I use **AI product videos for Shopee** and other shopping channels without creating a mismatch between the video and the product page?”

## What shopping platforms are automating

The exact features vary by platform and can change over time. CNA reported that Shopee introduced a video feature in late 2025 to give shoppers additional product information. The report also said TikTok Shop had updated its video maker tool so creators could automatically generate AI videos with embedded product links.

Those features can help expand the amount of shopping content available to consumers. They can also create a gap between three parties:

- The **seller**, who knows the product and controls the listing.
- The **creator or affiliate**, who may publish content that links to the product.
- The **platform**, which provides tools, recommendations, and safeguards.

A seller can be affected by a video even when the seller did not write the script or generate the visuals. In CNA’s reporting, a Shopee spokesperson said product videos could include user-contributed content, including AI-generated content, and encouraged sellers to report concerns for review. The report also described sellers’ concerns about whether they could remove or replace videos and how quickly changes would appear.

This makes monitoring part of the content strategy. Sellers should not assume that the product page is the only representation customers will see.

## The four accuracy failures to check first

A useful review begins with the errors most likely to change a shopper’s expectation. These checks apply whether a video was made by a seller, an affiliate, an agency, or an automated platform tool.

### 1. Product identity

Does the item shown match the item in the listing? Check the model, packaging, logo, cover, shape, materials, and important design details. Generated visuals can introduce attractive but incorrect changes. If the product has several variants, make sure the video does not combine details from two different versions.

### 2. Scale and proportions

A product should appear at a believable size relative to a person or surrounding object. A calendar that looks unusually large, for example, can create a false expectation before delivery. Show a reliable reference when size matters, and use measurements from the product information rather than relying on generated perspective.

### 3. Colour and appearance

Lighting can affect how a product looks, but a video should not silently turn one colour into another. Review packaging, fabric, print, finish, and small visual details. Use approved photographs or footage whenever the exact appearance is important to the buying decision.

### 4. Claims and context

Read the script, captions, voiceover, and on-screen text. A product can be placed in a story that changes its meaning. CNA reported that a book dealing with emotional and verbal abuse was described in an AI-generated video as a “fun” story. Even if the title and link are correct, the description can still be misleading.

Ask whether every factual statement can be supported by the product page, specifications, or an approved source. Do not allow a generated tool to invent reviews, results, certifications, ingredients, guarantees, or product uses.

## A seller-controlled AI video workflow

The safest workflow gives a human owner responsibility at each stage. AI can accelerate production, but it should not be the final authority on what a product does or looks like.

### Step 1: Create a product fact sheet

Before generating anything, collect the information the video is allowed to use:

- Product name, variant, and SKU or internal identifier.
- Exact dimensions, materials, colour names, and included items.
- Approved benefits and prohibited or unsupported claims.
- Current price, promotion, delivery information, and terms.
- Target audience and intended shopping platform.
- Approved product images, footage, music, logos, and other assets.

This fact sheet becomes the source of truth for the script and review. Keep it separate from creative suggestions so that a catchy idea cannot overwrite a product fact.

### Step 2: Choose the right role for AI

AI may be useful for drafting a hook, arranging a storyboard, adapting captions, trimming footage, or creating a non-essential background. It should not be asked to guess a product’s physical details or manufacture a customer testimonial.

For high-trust categories, use real demonstrations and real evidence wherever possible. A generated visual can support the story, but the buyer should not mistake an illustration for proof of performance.

### Step 3: Generate a small set of variations

Start with a few clear concepts. Vary one element at a time, such as the opening hook, audience context, or call to action. Avoid generating a large library of unreviewed videos. More files do not create more value if the seller cannot check them.

### Step 4: Review the exported video

Review the actual file that will appear on the platform. Check the first frame, product shots, voiceover, captions, link, and final call to action. Look for spelling errors, strange hands or objects, incorrect text, audio problems, and sudden changes in the product.

### Step 5: Publish with a record

Save the approved file, source assets, prompt or brief, reviewer, date, product version, and destination. If a customer reports a problem later, the record helps the team identify which asset ran and what needs to be corrected.

## How to protect brand trust when affiliates create content

Affiliate content can introduce useful reach, but it also introduces a review challenge. CNA reported that one design-studio owner contacted creators directly after finding AI-generated videos that showed her products inaccurately. Some creators removed their videos, while others argued that they had the right to create content on their own profiles.

The seller’s practical response is to define a content policy before a problem occurs. The policy can explain:

- Which product images, logos, names, and claims affiliates may use.
- Whether AI-generated people, voices, or scenes are allowed.
- How product size, colour, and features must be represented.
- What counts as a prohibited testimonial or unsupported result.
- How affiliates should submit content for approval when approval is required.
- Where sellers and creators should report inaccurate or outdated videos.

Use written guidance that is easy to follow. A complicated policy that no creator reads will not protect the brand. A short list of approved claims and visual rules is more useful than vague instructions to “be authentic.”

Sellers should also monitor search results, product listings, affiliate profiles, and customer comments. If a video is inaccurate, document it with the URL or screen capture, report it through the available platform process, and communicate the correction clearly to customers when necessary.

## Measuring quality alongside video output

Automation makes production volume easy to count, but output volume is not the same as ecommerce performance. A useful measurement system connects the video to shopper behaviour and customer expectations.

Depending on the channel, track:

- Video views and meaningful engagement.
- Clicks to the product page.
- Add-to-cart and purchase activity under the agreed attribution model.
- Customer questions about product size, colour, contents, or features.
- Returns, complaints, or negative feedback linked to unclear creative.
- Approval time and the number of revisions.
- Which hooks and demonstrations are accurate and useful for shoppers.

Do not label a video a winner only because it receives attention. If it drives clicks by showing an exaggerated product, it may create poor-quality traffic and disappointment after purchase. A strong AI product video should help the right shopper understand the real product well enough to make an informed decision.

## When not to use fully generated product videos

Some use cases need more real-world evidence than a generated video can reliably provide. Be cautious with:

- Medical, health, beauty, and financial claims.
- Before-and-after results.
- Customer reviews and testimonials.
- Safety instructions and technical demonstrations.
- Luxury products where authenticity and material quality are central.
- Products whose size, texture, fit, or colour is the main buying consideration.
- Celebrity likenesses, voices, or endorsements.

AI can still support scripting, editing, translations, captions, or format adaptation in these campaigns. The important distinction is between using AI to assist production and using synthetic content to imply evidence that does not exist.

## A pre-publication checklist for AI product videos

Before publishing or approving a video, a seller should be able to answer “yes” to each of these questions:

- Is the exact product and variant represented?
- Are size, scale, colour, and packaging accurate?
- Are all claims supported by approved product information?
- Does the video avoid invented testimonials and endorsements?
- Are the music, images, voices, logos, and people used with appropriate permission?
- Are captions and translations accurate?
- Is the product link correct and is the offer still current?
- Has a person reviewed the complete exported video?
- Can the seller report, pause, or correct the asset if it creates confusion?
- Is there a record of the approved version and its publication location?

A short checklist helps a team move quickly without treating speed as a substitute for accountability.

## FAQ: AI product videos for Shopee and TikTok

### Can sellers stop every AI-generated video from appearing with their products?

Platform controls differ, and the available options can change. Sellers should review current settings, monitor their listings, and use the platform’s reporting or support process when a video is inaccurate or unauthorised.

### Are AI product videos automatically misleading?

No. AI can assist with useful, accurate content. The risk comes from unreviewed generation, including incorrect product details, unsupported claims, misleading context, or visuals that create a false expectation.

### Should sellers use real product footage?

When appearance, scale, texture, fit, or performance matters, real product footage is often the clearest evidence. AI can help with editing and adaptation while the core product representation remains grounded in approved assets.

### How many videos should a seller create?

There is no universal number. Start with a manageable set of variations tied to clear hypotheses, then keep the versions that are accurate and useful. A smaller approved library is better than a large set of confusing assets.
