## A new AI UGC ad generator workflow puts the script first

AI video tools are making it easier to turn a simple brief into a short, presenter-led concept. On October 2, Magic Hour announced a free AI UGC ad workflow using the LTX-2.3 model. Its announcement describes a clip made from an actor image and a script, up to three seconds long at 480p. Magic Hour’s own product page lays out the broader workflow: choose or upload an actor, set the opening shot, then add the dialogue to generate an ad.

That is a small but useful development for marketers who want to preview a hook or visual direction before investing in a more complete production. It is not a finished campaign plan, proof that an ad will perform, or a substitute for checking usage rights. Think of a short AI UGC video as a **creative concept to review**, not an automatic shortcut from prompt to profitable campaign.

## What an AI UGC ad generator does

An AI UGC ad generator creates a user-generated-content-style video from inputs such as a presenter image, product framing, and spoken script. In Magic Hour’s described flow, users can choose or upload an actor image, set the pose, background, and product for the opening frame, and enter dialogue. The new LTX-2.3 free workflow is narrower: Magic Hour says it generates a clip up to three seconds at 480p from an image and script.

The “UGC-style” label describes the format or visual approach; it does not mean that a real customer independently used the product or supplied an authentic testimonial. That distinction matters. A synthetic presenter should not be given a made-up personal story, review, or result that viewers could mistake for a real customer’s experience.

Because the free output is only a few seconds, it can be most useful for previewing a single moment: a product reveal, a brief opening line, or a visual hook. A complete ad may need additional footage, editing, sound, captions, a clear offer, and context. The generated clip is one possible ingredient in that process.

## A six-step workflow for a useful first draft

### 1. Start with one verifiable message

Write down the product fact or benefit that the video should communicate. Keep it specific and supportable. Avoid unsupported performance promises, health claims, comparisons, or customer outcomes. If the message depends on a condition—such as eligibility, pricing, or a limited offer—make sure the final creative includes that context clearly.

### 2. Prepare an image you have permission to use

Choose an actor or image that suits the audience and campaign. If you upload a person’s photo, confirm that you have permission to use it for the intended type of content. Check product photos, packaging, logos, and other supplied assets in the same way. A free generation option does not automatically establish that every input is cleared for every commercial use.

### 3. Write a line that fits the clip

A three-second output leaves little room for a long script. Draft one short line focused on one point, then read it aloud at a natural pace before generating. Avoid writing fake first-person endorsements such as “I tried this and…” unless the depicted speaker actually made that statement and you can substantiate it.

### 4. Give the opening frame a clear job

For the broader product workflow, Magic Hour describes setting the pose, background, and product in the opening shot. Make those choices intentional: the product should be easy to identify, the presenter should not obscure it, and the background should support rather than distract from the message. Review the output rather than assuming the generated frame will match every detail in the brief.

### 5. Inspect the result before editing or publishing

Watch the clip carefully. Check the product’s shape, label, color, logo, and placement; the presenter’s expression and movement; and whether the visual supports the exact words in the script. Look for distorted text, unexpected objects, awkward timing, or any mismatch between the image and the spoken claim. Regenerate or edit when the result could confuse a customer.

### 6. Add context and test a real creative hypothesis

A short clip may need editing into the required aspect ratio and placement, as well as captions, a call to action, or additional scenes. Create variations only when each one tests a distinct idea—such as a different opening line or product angle. Then review the finished creative under the normal advertising process for the channel where it will appear. A generated video is an asset, not evidence of audience response; performance has to be measured after launch.

## Check usage rights and disclosure before using an output

“Free to generate” and “free for commercial use” are not necessarily the same thing. On the Magic Hour product page reviewed for this article, the company says subscriptions and credit-pack purchases permit commercial use of outputs, while users without either purchase are limited to personal, non-commercial use. Review the current terms and the account-specific settings before putting an output into an ad, sponsored post, or other business campaign. Product terms can change, so confirm them directly before publishing.

Also decide how to handle synthetic presenters in a way that is accurate and transparent for the campaign. Do not present a generated person as a real customer or imply personal experience that did not occur. Review the applicable platform policies and local requirements for synthetic or altered media, endorsements, and advertising claims. This is a practical review step, not legal advice.

Whether the final result is worth using still depends on its accuracy, audience fit, rights, editing needs, and performance in the intended campaign.

## FAQ: AI UGC ad generators

### What is an AI UGC ad generator?

It is a tool for creating UGC-style video ads with AI-generated or selected presenters and supplied creative inputs such as a script or image. Features and output limits vary by product.

### How long is the new free LTX-2.3 UGC clip?

Magic Hour’s announcement and changelog describe a free workflow that creates a clip up to three seconds at 480p. Check its product page for current usage limits and settings.

### Can I use a free AI UGC video in a paid ad?

Check the provider’s current terms. Magic Hour’s product page says commercial use is permitted with a subscription or credit pack, while users without either are limited to personal, non-commercial use.

### Will AI UGC ads automatically improve campaign performance?

No tool can guarantee that from generating a clip alone. Review the message and creative, then evaluate audience response through the campaign’s normal measurement process.
