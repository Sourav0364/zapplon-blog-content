## What Google Ads management costs for a small business

Most small businesses pay between **$500 and $2,000 a month** to have Google Ads managed, on top of the money spent on the ads themselves. Agencies that charge a percentage usually take **10% to 20% of monthly ad spend**. Reliablesoft puts the typical small business range at $500 to $1,500 a month. Outer Box Design lists $500 to $2,000 for accounts spending $1,000 to $5,000 a month on ads.

Those are two separate bills. If you spend $3,000 on clicks and pay $600 to a manager, your real monthly cost is $3,600. Any quote that blurs the two is hard to compare.

Fees go up with ad spend and complexity. Outer Box Design shows roughly $1,500 to $5,000 a month in management for accounts spending $5,000 to $25,000 on ads. The rest of this guide explains what sits behind those numbers and how to avoid overpaying.

## The four ways agencies charge

Agencies and freelancers use four pricing models. Each suits a different kind of business.

- **Flat monthly fee.** You pay the same amount each month, typically $500 to $5,000 or more for local businesses, according to ClicksGeek. This is the easiest to budget for. It works best when your ad spend is under about $3,000 a month.
- **Percentage of ad spend.** Typically 10% to 20%. On $5,000 of spend, that is $500 to $1,000. It scales with your budget, but it also means the manager earns more when you spend more, whether or not results improve.
- **Hybrid.** A smaller flat fee plus a smaller percentage. This is common once spend passes a few thousand dollars.
- **Hourly or project-based.** Better for a one-off audit, a clean-up of a messy account, or training your own staff than for ongoing management. Outer Box Design makes the same point.

Setup fees are also common. Ask whether the first month includes account build, conversion tracking and landing page advice, or whether those are extra.

## What drives the price up or down

Two quotes for the same ad budget can differ by hundreds of dollars. The usual reasons are below.

- **Scope.** A single Search campaign for one service is cheap to run. Search plus Performance Max plus Shopping plus call tracking is not.
- **Number of locations or service lines.** A plumber covering three cities needs more campaigns than a single-location dentist.
- **Reporting and calls.** Weekly calls and custom dashboards cost more than a monthly email summary.
- **Who does the work.** A freelancer is usually cheaper than an agency. A large agency often has minimum fees that suit larger budgets.
- **Your industry.** Competitive categories need more active management because clicks cost more.

If a proposal does not say exactly which of these are included, ask before you sign.

## What the ads themselves will cost you

The management fee is only part of the picture. Clicks are the larger cost. WordStream's 2025 Google Ads benchmarks put the average cost per click across industries at **$5.26**, up 12.88% on the previous year. Costs vary a lot by trade:

- Restaurants and food: about $2.05
- Dentists and dental services: about $7.85
- Home and home improvement: about $7.85
- Attorneys and legal services: about $8.58

These are averages across the US. Your cost in a competitive California city can be higher. The same report covers cost per lead by industry, which is a better number to plan around than cost per click.

A rough example: at $5.26 a click, $1,000 buys under 200 clicks. If a few percent of those become enquiries, you are looking at a handful of leads. That is why the ratio of fee to ad spend matters so much.

## Match the fee to your ad budget

Management fees should not swallow your budget. Reliablesoft gives a blunt example: spend $608 on ads and pay $500 for management, and almost half your money goes to the manager. Not much is left to produce leads.

As a rule of thumb for your situation:

- **Under $1,000 a month in ad spend.** A full-service agency is rarely worth it. Consider learning the basics yourself, paying a freelancer for a one-off setup and a quarterly check, or putting the money into your Google Business Profile and local SEO instead.
- **$1,000 to $3,000 a month.** A flat fee of $500 to $1,000 is realistic. Make sure the scope is limited to what you need.
- **$3,000 to $10,000 a month.** Percentage or hybrid models make sense. Expect 10% to 20%, and expect more hands-on optimisation and reporting.
- **Above $10,000 a month.** Fees often drop towards the lower end of the percentage range, and you can negotiate performance-linked terms.

A sensible target is a management fee that sits at no more than about 20% to 30% of what you spend on ads. If it is higher, the budget is probably too small for the service you are buying.

## What good management actually includes

You are paying for judgement, not for someone to press buttons. A competent monthly service should include:

- **Conversion tracking set up correctly.** Phone calls, form fills and booked appointments, not just clicks. Without this, nobody can tell you what works.
- **Negative keyword management.** Blocking irrelevant searches is one of the biggest ways to stop wasted spend.
- **Ad copy and landing page testing.** New versions tested against old ones, with results explained.
- **Budget and bid adjustments.** Based on cost per lead, not on impressions.
- **Clear monthly reporting.** Spend, leads, cost per lead and what changes were made, in plain English.

If a proposal lists "campaign optimisation" without any of the above, ask what that means in practice.

## Red flags when you hire

Most bad experiences come from a short list of problems.

- **No access to your own account.** The ad account must be in your business name. If the agency runs it under theirs, you may lose your history when you leave.
- **Long lock-in contracts.** Six or twelve months with no exit is a risk. Month-to-month or a 90-day minimum is reasonable.
- **Guaranteed rankings or leads.** Nobody can guarantee a result in an auction. Treat that promise as a warning.
- **Hidden markups.** Some agencies buy clicks and resell them. Ask whether your ad spend goes directly to Google.
- **Vague reporting.** If you cannot see lead numbers and cost per lead, you cannot judge the work.
- **Reporting on clicks and impressions only.** These are not business results.

## Is paying for management worth it

It depends on what a lead is worth to you. Do the sum before you hire anyone. Take your average job or sale value, the share of enquiries you close, and the cost per lead you can afford.

Say a customer is worth $800 in profit and you close one in four enquiries. You could afford up to about $200 per lead and still break even. If your cost per lead is $90, a $3,000 ad budget gives about 33 leads (the example used by Reliablesoft). That is a strong return even after a $600 management fee. If a lead is worth $40 in profit, the same numbers do not work and paid search is the wrong channel.

Doing it yourself is a fair option if you have time and a small budget. The risk is that the platform pushes you towards broad settings and automated campaigns that spend quickly. A few hours of setup advice from a specialist can be cheaper than a full retainer.

Agencies that cover several channels, such as Zapplon's [lead generation and advertising services](https://www.zapplon.com/services), can make sense if you also need local SEO or Meta ads. A specialist freelancer is often a better fit if Google Ads is all you need and your budget is modest.

## Questions to ask before you sign

Put these to every provider and compare the answers side by side.

1. What is the total monthly cost, including setup and any tool fees?
2. Will the ad account be in my business name, and will I have admin access?
3. How will you track phone calls and form submissions?
4. What is the minimum contract, and how do I cancel?
5. Who will actually manage my account day to day?
6. What cost per lead is realistic in my area and trade, and on what evidence?
7. What will I see in each monthly report?

A good provider answers these directly. If the answers are vague, treat that as information.

## FAQ

### How much should a small business pay for Google Ads management?

Most pay $500 to $2,000 a month, or 10% to 20% of ad spend, depending on budget and scope. Smaller budgets generally suit a flat fee. The management fee is separate from the ad spend that goes to Google.

### Is 20% of ad spend a fair management fee?

It is at the top of the usual range. It can be fair on smaller budgets, where the work is the same regardless of spend. On larger budgets, ask for a lower rate or a hybrid fee. Look at the cost per lead the agency delivers rather than the percentage alone.

### What is the minimum Google Ads budget worth managing?

There is no official minimum, but a fee of $500 or more on a few hundred dollars of ad spend rarely pays off. Many providers expect at least $1,000 to $2,000 a month in spend. Below that, your money is usually better used on a one-off setup or on local SEO.

## Next step

Work out what a lead is worth to you, then get two or three written quotes that separate fees from ad spend. Choose the provider who is clearest about tracking, contracts and account ownership, not the one with the lowest price.
