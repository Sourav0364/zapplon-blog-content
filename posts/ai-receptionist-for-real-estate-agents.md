## What an AI receptionist does for a real estate agent

An AI receptionist for real estate is a voice system that answers your business phone, talks to the caller, collects their details and books a showing or call-back. It works at 2 pm during an open house and at 10 pm on a Sunday. Published entry prices from vendors start at roughly **$14 to $99 per month** for basic call answering. Setups that connect to your CRM and calendar usually cost more, and the final figure depends on call volume.

Those entry prices come from vendor pricing pages, including the pricing page at myaifrontdesk.com, which lists plans from about $14 a month up to $99 a month for a phone-answering plan. Treat them as a starting point, not a quote. Included minutes, integrations and setup fees change the real bill.

The short version: if buyers and sellers call you while you are driving, showing a home or in a signing, an AI receptionist can stop those calls going to voicemail. It will not replace your judgment or your relationship with a client.

## Why missed calls cost real estate agents money

Most agents work alone or in a small team. You cannot answer the phone during a showing, a listing appointment or a closing. A caller who reaches voicemail often calls the next agent on the list.

Real estate calls are also time-sensitive. A buyer who sees a listing on Zillow or Instagram and calls usually wants an answer now. A seller comparing agents may speak to three people in one afternoon. The first agent who picks up and sounds organised has the advantage.

Exact figures on how many calls agents miss vary by market and team size, and no single number applies to you. You can find your own number in about a week:

- Check your phone log for the last 30 days and count unanswered calls.
- Mark which were from new numbers, not existing clients.
- Estimate how many of those would have become a showing or listing conversation.
- Multiply by your average commission to see what a missed call could cost.

Even a rough estimate tells you whether a monthly fee of under $100 or a few hundred dollars is small or large next to the lost business.

## How it works on a typical buyer call

When a call comes in, the system answers in your brand name. It can speak to a buyer asking about a listing, a seller wanting a valuation or a renter asking about availability.

A typical call goes like this:

1. **Greeting.** The AI introduces your office and asks how it can help.
2. **Questions.** It asks the caller's name, number, the property or area, budget range, timeline and whether they are working with another agent.
3. **Answers.** It can give basic information you have loaded in, such as listing price, open house times, your service areas and how you work.
4. **Next step.** It books a showing on your calendar, offers a call-back time or transfers the call to you if you are free.
5. **Summary.** You get a text or email with the caller's details and a short transcript.

## What it costs, and what drives the price

Pricing generally follows one of three models.

- **Flat monthly plan.** A set number of minutes or calls for a fixed fee. Predictable, but overage charges apply if you go past the limit.
- **Per-minute pricing.** You pay for talk time. This suits low call volume and gets expensive during a busy listing launch.
- **Custom build.** A provider sets up the agent around your scripts, CRM and calendar, and charges a setup fee plus a monthly fee.

What pushes the cost up:

- Call volume, especially if you run paid ads that drive a lot of calls
- Connecting to a CRM such as Follow Up Boss or kvCORE, or to your MLS leads
- Several agents or phone lines on one team
- Custom scripts for different listings
- Text follow-up, not just voice

Compare this with a human alternative. A live answering service typically charges per minute or per call, and a part-time assistant is a payroll cost. An AI receptionist is usually cheaper than both, but it is also less flexible. Ask every provider for a written price for your expected call volume, and ask what happens if you go over.

## What it can handle, and what it should not

Be realistic about the limits. An AI receptionist is good at routine, repeatable calls. It is weaker when a call needs empathy, negotiation or local knowledge.

**Good fit for AI:**

- After-hours and weekend calls
- Calls during showings and open houses
- Sign calls and listing enquiries
- Collecting buyer details and pre-approval status
- Booking showings and listing consultations
- Answering "is it still available" questions

**Keep for yourself:**

- Price negotiation and offer discussions
- Anything involving legal advice or contract terms
- Upset clients or disputes in the middle of a transaction
- Advice on whether to buy or sell
- Sensitive situations such as a probate or divorce sale

The AI should not give legal, financial or fair housing advice, and it should not steer callers based on who they are. Set it up so it sticks to your script and passes anything outside it to you. Fair housing rules apply to your communications, and that includes automated ones. Do not let it comment on neighbourhood demographics or school quality in a way that could be seen as steering. Test for this before you go live.

## Legal points to check in California and elsewhere

Call recording and transcripts are the main legal question. California generally requires all parties to consent to recording a call under Penal Code section 632, so your AI should tell callers at the start that the call may be recorded or transcribed. Other states have different rules, so check the one where you work. This is general information, not legal advice. Ask your broker or a lawyer to review your greeting.

Outbound calls are a separate issue. In 2024 the FCC ruled that AI-generated voices count as artificial voices under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, which means calling people with an AI voice generally needs their prior consent. An AI receptionist that answers inbound calls is lower risk than one that cold-calls. If a provider suggests AI cold calling your old leads, ask them how they handle consent and the Do Not Call list before agreeing.

Also check your brokerage's policies. Some require approved scripts or disclosure language, and some control how lead data is stored.

## How to set one up in a week

You do not need a long project. Most agents can be live in a few days if they prepare the following.

1. **Write your script.** Decide the questions the AI asks and the answers it can give.
2. **List your facts.** Service areas, commission-neutral descriptions of how you work, current listings, open house times.
3. **Connect your calendar.** The AI can only book showings if it sees your real availability.
4. **Choose where leads go.** Text, email or CRM. Pick one place you actually check.
5. **Set transfer rules.** Decide which calls ring through to your cell, such as existing clients or a named seller.
6. **Add the recording notice.** Put the disclosure in the greeting.
7. **Test with friends.** Call it ten times with awkward questions, accents and interruptions.

After launch, listen to the first 20 to 30 calls. Fix wrong answers, and adjust the script. Plan to review calls weekly for the first month.

## How to decide if it is right for you

Use this quick test.

- **You probably benefit** if you miss several calls a week, you run ads or yard signs that generate calls, or you work solo or in a small team.
- **You probably do not need it** if you have a full-time assistant who answers every call, or you get only a few calls a month.
- **A cheaper option may be enough** if the real problem is voicemail. A good voicemail greeting plus an automatic text-back ("I am with a client, what can I help with?") costs almost nothing and may handle a quiet practice.

A sensible approach is a one-month trial on a monthly plan with no long contract. Measure answered calls, showings booked and leads that came from calls that would otherwise have been missed. If the numbers do not justify the fee after 30 to 60 days, cancel.

Questions to ask any provider:

- Is there a contract, and can I cancel monthly?
- Which CRM and calendar tools do you connect with?
- Who owns the call recordings and lead data?
- What does the AI do when it does not know an answer?
- Can I hear real call samples from real estate clients?

## FAQ

### How much does an AI receptionist for real estate cost?

Published entry plans from vendors start at around $14 to $99 per month for basic call answering. A setup that connects to your CRM, handles several agents or takes a high call volume costs more. Always get a written quote based on your call numbers.

### Will callers know they are talking to an AI?

Many will notice, and you should not hide it. Some states and platforms are moving toward disclosure rules, and being open builds trust. Most callers care more about getting a quick answer and a booked showing than about who answered.

### Can an AI receptionist replace my assistant or transaction coordinator?

No. It covers the phone: answering, qualifying and booking. It does not manage paperwork, deadlines, negotiations or client relationships. Use it to take pressure off the phone, not to replace people who do the transaction work.
