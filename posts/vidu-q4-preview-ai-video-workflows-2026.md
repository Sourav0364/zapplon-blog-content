## What is Vidu Q4 Preview?

Shengshu Technology announced **Vidu Q4 Preview** on October 7, 2026, as the first public preview of its next-generation flagship model for expressive audio and video, according to the [company’s launch announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/vidu-launches-q4-preview-making-flagship-ai-video-creation-accessible-to-everyone-302901266.html) and an [independent report from Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/vidu-releases-q4-preview-of-next-generation-flagship-ai-video-model/). The company says it is available through Vidu’s web product and API platform, and is intended for independent creators, small studios, and production teams working on narrative and commercial projects.

For marketers, the useful question is not whether a new model can make a striking demo. It is whether its controls fit a real production task: creating several on-brand concepts, keeping a character or product recognizable, and making revisions without losing the brief. Vidu’s announcement emphasizes reference-guided generation, character performance, camera movement, and visual effects. These capabilities may be worth evaluating for social campaigns, product storytelling, or short-form ad concepts, but every output still needs human review.

The release is a **preview**, so teams should check current availability, plan limits, and usage terms before building a workflow around it. Capabilities described by the developer are product claims, not independent guarantees of a particular creative or campaign outcome.

## Reference inputs give teams more creative direction

Vidu Q4 Preview supports up to 15 image references and up to three audio references, according to the launch announcement. Shengshu describes image references as a way to guide details such as characters, wardrobe, props, products, and environments. Audio references can help guide voice consistency and emotional delivery.

This can make a difference when a marketing team needs a video to stay connected to approved assets. A team might use reference images to establish a product’s look, a character’s wardrobe, or the intended environment, then compare alternative prompts for different scenes. Audio references may be useful when voice continuity is central to a creative concept.

These inputs are guidance, not a substitute for checking the final result. Review whether the generated subject still matches the product or brand, whether voice and visuals remain appropriate, and whether any supplied material is cleared for use. Before upload, confirm your rights to every reference image and audio clip, including any music or spoken performance.

## Resolution, color, and production considerations

The announcement lists 2K and 4K output options with 10-bit color depth. Higher resolution can be useful when a production needs more room for framing, cropping, or finishing, but choosing the highest setting is not automatically the best first step. A low-cost or faster concept draft may be enough to evaluate the story, hook, and visual direction before spending time on a final version.

A sensible early workflow separates **concept approval** from **delivery quality**:

- Draft a concise prompt around one audience, one message, and one action.
- Use reference assets only when they help clarify the desired subject, setting, or sound.
- Review a draft for story and accuracy before requesting a higher-resolution output.
- Decide what aspect ratio and final dimensions the destination requires.
- Keep a record of the prompt, references, model settings, and approved output.

The exact supported settings can change between the web product, API, subscription plans, and regions. Check the current Vidu interface or documentation rather than assuming every option is available everywhere.

## Where Vidu Q4 may fit in an ad workflow

Shengshu positions the preview for both narrative and commercial production. That makes it relevant for teams exploring short-form advertising, but a general video model does not replace a campaign plan. Start with the advertising problem, not the model: what should viewers understand, remember, or do after watching?

Potential test concepts could include a product demonstration, a scene that illustrates a customer use case, or a short narrative that makes a service benefit easier to grasp. Keep product details and claims faithful to the actual offer. If the video includes people, voices, or recognizable creative elements, ensure that the source assets and generated result meet the business’s rights, disclosure, and brand requirements.

A controlled pilot can be structured as follows:

1. **Choose a single objective**, such as explaining a product feature or testing a new opening hook.
2. **Write a brief** with the audience, message, tone, call to action, and prohibited claims.
3. **Prepare reference material** that the team is authorized to use and that accurately represents the product or brand.
4. **Generate a small number of variations** that test a meaningful creative difference instead of changing everything at once.
5. **Review each output** for accuracy, voice, visual continuity, captions, and suitability for the destination.
6. **Test the approved versions** using a measurement plan tied to the campaign objective.

This makes it easier to learn whether an AI-generated treatment is useful for your audience. Do not assume a visually impressive clip will outperform existing creative; treat performance as something to measure.

## What creative teams should check before adopting it

A new model can introduce opportunities as well as new review work. Before rolling Vidu Q4 Preview into a recurring workflow, consider these practical questions:

- **Access:** Is the required feature available in your account, web product, or API plan today?
- **Repeatability:** Can the team get sufficiently consistent characters, products, and tone across the shots it needs?
- **Editing:** Does the output fit the team’s existing editing and approval process, or will extra finishing work be required?
- **Cost and time:** What is the total cost of generating, revising, reviewing, and finishing each usable asset—not just one generation?
- **Rights and policy:** Are the inputs cleared, and does the final material meet platform, legal, and company requirements?
- **Measurement:** What campaign or production metric will tell you whether the workflow is useful?

These checks help separate an interesting preview from an operationally sound tool choice. If a model is still changing, keep the pilot small and document any limitations discovered by the team.

## How to evaluate results without overclaiming

Compare AI-generated work with an appropriate baseline. That could be an existing static ad, a traditionally produced video, or another approved variation, depending on the question you are testing. Keep the audience, offer, destination, and measurement method as consistent as possible so that you can interpret the difference.

Look at the signals that fit the goal. A brand-awareness concept and a direct-response ad should not be judged by one identical measure. Review the actual campaign objective, conversion event, and reporting window before drawing a conclusion. If the sample is small or the test conditions changed, describe the result as directional rather than definitive.

Also save examples of failure. A model may misrepresent packaging, alter a visual detail, or produce an expression or movement that does not match the brief. Capturing these issues gives the team a more realistic picture of review effort and helps improve future prompts and source selection.
