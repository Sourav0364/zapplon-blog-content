## What missed calls cost a small business

For most small service businesses, the cost of missed calls is the value of the jobs those callers would have booked. Take your missed calls per month, multiply by the share that were new customers, then by your close rate and average job value. A business that misses 40 calls a month, with half of them new enquiries, a 40% close rate and a $500 average job, loses about **$4,000 a month**, or $48,000 a year.

Your own figure could be a fraction of that or several times higher. The rest of this post shows how to calculate it with your real numbers, what is realistic to recover, and which fix suits your size of business.

## What the published statistics say, and how far to trust them

You will see figures like "62% of calls to small service businesses go unanswered" and "85% of callers who miss you never call back". A statistics roundup on SchedulingKit attributes the first to a BIA/Kelsey local commerce report and the second to a Keap small business report. The same roundup puts the average cost of a missed call at $100 to $200.

Treat those numbers as directional, not as your reality. They come from a secondary roundup, the original studies were not checked here, and your own rate could be far lower or higher. A business with a full-time front desk may miss 10% of calls. A two-person plumbing crew working under sinks may miss most of them.

The reliable number is your own, and your phone system can tell you what it is.

## How to count your own missed calls

Pull your call log for the last 30 days. Most business phone systems, and most mobile carriers, let you export it. Google Business Profile also shows call data if calls are tracked.

Count these:

- **Total inbound calls** during opening hours
- **Unanswered calls**, including ones that went to voicemail
- **Calls after hours**, which are easy to forget
- **Voicemails left**, and how many you returned the same day

Then sample 20 of the missed numbers. Call them back or search them in your customer list. You are looking for how many were existing customers, how many were spam or sales calls, and how many were genuinely new people asking for a quote or appointment. Only the last group is lost revenue.

## The formula, with worked examples

Use this:

**Missed new-customer calls per month × close rate × average job value = monthly revenue lost**

Use your gross job value for the first pass, then margin if you want a more conservative figure. Here are three illustrative examples. The inputs are assumptions for the sake of the arithmetic, not industry benchmarks.

- **Plumber or HVAC company:** 30 missed new-customer calls, 50% close rate, $450 average job. That is **$6,750 a month**. Emergency callers ring the next company on the list within minutes, so close rate on answered calls tends to be high.
- **Dental or medical practice:** 20 missed new-patient calls, 35% would book, $1,200 first-year patient value. That is **$8,400 a month**. The value of a patient over several years is higher, but use first-year value to stay cautious.
- **Salon or med spa:** 25 missed booking calls, 60% would book, $120 average ticket. That is **$1,800 a month**, before repeat visits.

Even the smallest of these is more than the cost of most answering solutions. That is the point of doing the sum before deciding anything.

## Why calls get missed in the first place

Fixing the problem starts with knowing which cause applies to you. They need different solutions.

- **You are busy with a customer.** Common for contractors, salons and clinics. The phone rings while your hands are occupied.
- **Peak times overlap.** One receptionist can handle one call. Two calls at 12:15 means one is missed. Monday mornings and lunch hours are typical crunch points.
- **After hours.** Many people search and call in the evening or at weekends. If you close at 5pm, those calls hit voicemail.
- **Staff turnover or sickness.** One absence at a small front desk can leave the phone uncovered.
- **Calls routed badly.** A phone tree, a ringing desk phone nobody hears, or a mobile on silent.

If most of your misses are after hours, you do not need another employee. If they are during peak times, you need overflow coverage.

## Your options, from cheapest to most complete

**1. Fix the basics (free to low cost).** Forward the office line to a mobile that someone watches. Set a voicemail greeting that promises a call back within a set time. Turn on missed-call text replies so callers get an immediate message. This costs little and recovers some calls, but it still depends on you responding quickly.

**2. A human answering service.** A live operator answers in your business name, takes details and passes them on. It works well for businesses that want a person on the line. Pricing is usually based on minutes or calls, and plans vary, so compare quotes. The limitation is that operators rarely know your pricing or calendar well enough to book jobs.

**3. A part-time or full-time receptionist.** Best when you have steady volume and want someone who also handles walk-ins and paperwork. It is the most expensive route, and it still leaves lunch breaks, sick days and after-hours gaps.

**4. An AI voice agent or AI receptionist.** Software answers every call, at any hour, can answer common questions and can book into your calendar. For businesses that miss calls because of overlap or after-hours gaps, [an AI voice agent](https://www.zapplon.com/ai-agents) can close those gaps at a lower cost than adding staff. Pricing varies by call volume and features, so ask for a quote based on your actual call count.

## When an AI receptionist is a poor fit

Be honest about the limits before you buy.

- **Emotionally sensitive calls.** A law firm taking a call from someone in crisis, or a clinic dealing with a worried patient, may want a human first.
- **Complicated, one-off enquiries.** If most of your calls are unique custom quotes with many follow-up questions, an agent can capture the details but cannot close the sale.
- **Very low call volume.** If you miss three new calls a month, the formula may show a loss too small to justify any paid system. Call forwarding and a text reply may be enough.
- **Regulated information.** Healthcare practices need to check how patient information is handled before they let any system take calls.
- **Callers who insist on a person.** Some older customers will hang up. Make sure the system can transfer to you or take a message cleanly.

The right test is simple. Run the formula, run the quote, and compare. If the quoted monthly cost is well below your monthly loss, and your calls are mostly routine, automation is worth a trial.

## How to decide and what to do this week

Work through this in order. It takes about an hour.

1. Export 30 days of call logs and count missed calls.
2. Sample 20 missed numbers and sort them into new customers, existing customers and spam.
3. Apply the formula to get your monthly loss.
4. Identify whether misses cluster at peak times, after hours or when staff are away.
5. Start with the free fixes today: forwarding, a better voicemail greeting and an automatic text reply.
6. Get two or three quotes for the option that matches your pattern, and ask for a trial period or monthly rolling contract.
7. Recount after 30 days. If the missed-call count has not dropped, the fix is not working.

Tracking matters as much as the fix. Without a before-and-after count, you will not know whether you recovered $500 a month or $5,000.

## FAQ

### How many missed calls are normal for a small business?

There is no reliable single benchmark. Widely repeated figures say well over half of calls to small service businesses go unanswered, but those come from secondary sources and vary by trade. Your own call log is more useful than any average.

### Do callers leave voicemails?

Many do not. Roundups of call statistics suggest most callers who reach voicemail hang up without leaving a message, and those who do may have called a competitor by the time you reply. Treat voicemail as a weak safety net, especially for urgent jobs like plumbing, HVAC or dental pain.

### What is the cheapest way to stop losing leads from missed calls?

Start with call forwarding to a mobile, a clear voicemail greeting and an automatic text reply to missed calls. These cost little or nothing. If your pattern is after-hours or overlapping calls, a paid answering service or AI receptionist is the next step, once your formula shows the loss is large enough to justify it.
