## The latest shift in AI video ads for CTV

Producing a useful video ad has traditionally involved several handoffs: a brief, a script, visual selection, voiceover, music, editing, and review. That workflow can make video difficult for smaller teams and can slow down marketers that need multiple creative versions.

A July 30, 2026 report from **Demand Gen Report** highlighted a new approach from Adwave. Its standalone **Wavemaker** platform uses the infrastructure behind Adwave’s CTV advertising technology to turn a text prompt, URL, uploaded file, or topic into a scripted, voiced, and scored video. The report says the platform typically produces a video in two to five minutes and does not require editing experience or a production crew.

This is a product launch, not proof that every automatically generated video will perform well. The practical lesson is more measured: AI is moving parts of video production into one workflow, while marketers still need to supply strategy, brand judgment, approvals, and distribution decisions.

## What Wavemaker does in the production workflow

According to the report, Wavemaker automates the main production stages from one input. It writes a script, assembles visuals, generates voiceover narration, and scores background music. The result is designed to be a finished video rather than a project that still needs an editing timeline and manual asset sourcing.

The platform accepts several kinds of starting material:

- **Natural-language prompts** describing an idea, offer, audience, or message.
- **URLs** that can provide a product or company page as the source material.
- **Documents and uploaded files** that contain information the video should use.
- **A topic** that gives the system a starting direction.

The report also says Wavemaker generates AI imagery, video clips, voiceover, and background music in parallel. This matters because a marketer can begin with information already available to the business instead of preparing every creative component separately.

Automation of these steps does not remove the need for review. A source document can be incomplete, a URL can contain information that is not suitable for an ad, and a generated script can miss a brand’s preferred tone. A fast draft is useful only when a person checks claims, product details, rights, and calls to action before publication.

## Why the CTV angle matters

Connected TV, or **CTV**, is television content delivered through internet-connected devices and services. An AI video generator built on CTV advertising infrastructure is notable because it connects content creation with a distribution environment rather than treating video as an isolated asset.

Demand Gen Report describes Wavemaker as a platform that allows small businesses to create and launch professional CTV commercials in minutes. That positioning is important for businesses that may not have an internal production team or the resources to coordinate a traditional commercial shoot.

The same report says the output supports **landscape, portrait, square, and 4:5 aspect ratios**. Those formats give a team more flexibility when a single creative idea needs to travel across different placements, including YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, and other major platforms. A marketer should still adapt the opening, length, text treatment, and call to action for each placement; changing the canvas alone does not make every version effective.

For CTV planning, the useful question is not simply, “Can AI make a video?” It is, “Can the team create an on-brand message, review it, and prepare the right version for the intended screen and audience?” A workflow that reduces production friction can make that question easier to answer, but it does not replace media planning or measurement.

## A practical workflow for marketers

Teams considering AI video ads for CTV can use a simple process that separates automation from accountability.

### 1. Start with one approved source

Choose a product page, brief, offer document, or campaign objective. Keep the source current and identify which statements are approved for public use. If the input contains pricing, dates, guarantees, or regulated claims, mark those items for a dedicated review.

### 2. Write the audience and action before the prompt

A video needs a job. The job might be introducing a product, explaining a service, or driving a viewer to a landing page. Define the audience and the desired next step first, then ask the tool to express that message. This prevents a visually polished video from becoming a collection of disconnected scenes.

### 3. Generate a first draft quickly

Use the prompt, URL, file, or topic to produce a draft. The value of a two-to-five-minute generation window is iteration: the team can explore alternate openings, voiceover approaches, and visual directions without treating the first output as final.

### 4. Review the content and brand fit

Check the script against the source. Review names, product features, prices, disclaimers, pronunciation, music, imagery, and the final call to action. Also check whether the video presents a clear idea in the first moments and whether the visual choices fit the brand.

### 5. Prepare the correct formats

Select the aspect ratio that matches the destination. Landscape can suit television-style placements, while portrait and square versions may be more appropriate for mobile feeds. A 4:5 version can also be useful where that placement is supported. Treat each export as a creative version, not merely a resized copy.

### 6. Test and learn responsibly

Keep a record of the version, source, audience, placement, and objective. Compare creative variations using the measurement setup already used for the campaign. Do not assume that faster production automatically creates better performance; the benefit is the ability to test thoughtfully and improve the message.

## API and MCP access for automated workflows

Wavemaker is reported to use a credit-based pricing model. The free tier includes 150 credits, and Pro and higher plans include **REST API** and **MCP (Model Context Protocol)** access. The report says developers can connect those plans to existing workflows, use tools such as Cursor and Claude Desktop, or call the API from a programming language.

This creates a second use case beyond a marketer manually generating one video. A business could connect approved product information to a repeatable creative workflow, with a human review step before anything is published. For example, a team might prepare a queue of product-page drafts, route the generated outputs to a reviewer, and then send approved versions to the relevant campaign process.

Integrations should be designed with boundaries. The system should know which sources it may read, which claims are approved, who can authorize an export, and where the final files are stored. API access can reduce repetitive work, but it also makes permissions, logging, and review controls more important.

## What this launch does—and does not—change

The Wavemaker launch is a sign that AI video generation is being packaged as a practical marketing workflow, not only as a creative experiment. A business can provide structured information and receive a multi-part video draft without separately organizing every production task.

However, several responsibilities remain with the marketing team:

- **Positioning:** The team must decide what the ad should communicate and to whom.
- **Accuracy:** Someone must verify that the script matches the source and does not make unsupported promises.
- **Brand safety:** Visuals, narration, music, and language need approval.
- **Rights and permissions:** Teams should confirm that assets and voices can be used for the intended campaign.
- **Distribution:** CTV, social, and video platforms have different specifications and audience contexts.
- **Measurement:** The campaign needs a defined objective and a way to assess outcomes.

The strongest implementation treats AI as a production assistant inside a controlled process. It accelerates drafts and variations while people remain responsible for the message and the decision to publish.

## FAQ: AI video ads for CTV

### What are AI video ads for CTV?

They are video advertisements created with generative or automated AI tools and prepared for connected-TV distribution. The tool may help with scripting, visuals, narration, music, or the production workflow, while the marketing team remains responsible for review and delivery.

### What is Adwave’s Wavemaker?

Wavemaker is a standalone AI video generation platform from Adwave. Demand Gen Report says it can turn a prompt, URL, document, uploaded file, or topic into a scripted, voiced, and scored video, and that it is built on infrastructure used for Adwave’s CTV advertising platform.

### How quickly can Wavemaker generate a video?

The July 30, 2026 report says videos are typically ready within five minutes and describes a two-to-five-minute generation window. Generation time should not be confused with the time needed for brand review, revisions, approvals, and campaign setup.

### Can one video work on every platform?

Not automatically. The report says Wavemaker supports landscape, portrait, square, and 4:5 aspect ratios for distribution across platforms such as YouTube, TikTok, and Instagram. Teams should still adapt the creative message and review requirements to each placement.

### Does AI video generation replace a marketing team?

No. It can automate production steps and make iteration easier, but strategy, factual review, brand approval, rights checks, media planning, and measurement still require responsible human decisions.
