## What an AI receptionist costs for a salon or med spa

An AI receptionist for a salon or med spa typically costs **$25 to $400 a month** for one location, depending on call volume and whether it books directly into your calendar. Most small salons land between $50 and $200 a month. A human front desk hire costs roughly $35,000 a year or more once payroll costs are included.

Published prices vary a lot between vendors. The AIRA blog lists plans from $24.95 a month for 30 calls up to $159.95 for 300 calls. SalonAgent says salons typically pay $80 to $400 a month. CloudTalk's 2026 review puts most single-location plans between $0 and $299 a month. These are vendor-published figures, so treat them as ranges for budgeting, not quotes.

The rest of this guide covers what the software does, what it cannot do, and how to decide.

## What it does when a client calls

An AI receptionist answers your phone in a natural voice, understands what the caller wants, and handles the routine requests. For a salon or med spa, that usually means:

- Booking, moving and cancelling appointments in your scheduling software
- Answering questions on prices, hours, parking and services
- Taking a message or transferring the call for anything it cannot handle
- Texting the caller a confirmation or a booking link
- Answering after hours, on days off and while every chair is busy

The last point matters most. Your stylist has colour on a client's head and cannot pick up. Your aesthetician is mid-treatment with gloves on. The call goes to voicemail, and many callers book with the next business on Google instead of leaving a message.

If you want to see how this kind of system is built and deployed, Zapplon explains it on its page about [AI voice agents and receptionists for small businesses](https://www.zapplon.com/ai-agents).

## Where the money comes from

The value is in the calls you currently miss, not in the salary you save. Vendors claim that salons miss a large share of calls at peak times, and that each missed call costs $55 to $80 in lost services. Those numbers come from a vendor blog (AIRA), not independent research, so check them against your own phone records.

You can work out your own number in ten minutes:

1. Look at your phone's missed call log for the last month.
2. Remove spam and existing clients who rebook online anyway.
3. Estimate how many of the rest were new clients wanting to book.
4. Multiply by your average first-visit ticket.

A med spa with a $350 average first treatment only needs to recover a handful of calls a month to cover a $200 plan. A budget nail salon with a $45 ticket needs far more, so the maths is tighter.

## Salon versus med spa: the differences that matter

The two businesses need different things from the same tool.

**Salons** have simple, repeat bookings. Cut, colour, nails, lashes. The AI needs to know which stylist does which service, how long each takes, and whether a colour consultation comes first. If your calendar already handles this logic, the AI mostly inherits it.

**Med spas** have more questions before a booking. Callers ask whether Botox hurts, whether they are a candidate for a laser treatment, or what happens if they are pregnant. A receptionist should not answer those. It should book a consultation and pass medical questions to a clinician.

Med spas also handle health information, which brings privacy rules into play. Before you buy, ask the vendor whether it will sign a business associate agreement and where call recordings are stored. Do not assume the answer is yes.

## How setup works and how long it takes

For simple tools, setup is quick. CloudTalk's review says basic tools can go live in minutes to a day, while human answering services take days to weeks for scripts and training. A more customised build takes longer.

You will typically need to do the following:

- **Connect your booking software.** Check the vendor supports yours. Common salon and spa systems include Vagaro, Square Appointments, Acuity, Boulevard and Zenoti, but support varies and some connections need a higher plan.
- **Write your service menu.** Include names, durations, prices and which staff member does what.
- **Set your rules.** Deposits, cancellation windows, patch tests, new client limits.
- **Choose how calls reach the AI.** Either forward your existing number, or move to a new phone system that includes it.
- **Decide when a human takes over.** Complaints, medical questions, VIP clients, anything unusual.

Plan on testing it yourself with 15 to 20 calls before it answers a real client. Try the awkward ones: a caller who changes their mind twice, a heavy accent, a noisy background, a request for a service you do not offer.

## What to watch out for

An AI receptionist is not right for every business. These are the common problems.

**Booking connections that are shallow.** Some tools take a message and email it to you. Others write directly into your calendar. Ask for a live demo of a booking appearing on your real calendar, not a slide.

**Pricing that grows with volume.** Plans are priced per minute, per call or per unique customer. Goodcall, for example, bills by unique customers per month, while Allo advertises a flat unlimited receptionist. Check what happens in your busiest week, such as the run-up to Christmas or prom season.

**Weak handling of complaints.** CloudTalk's comparison notes that a human service handles complaints and nuance better, and that AI must escalate those to a person. An upset client who reaches a bot with no way out will not return.

**Poor name and service recognition.** Names of treatments, brands and stylists can be misheard. Ask how the tool handles corrections and whether you can add a custom vocabulary.

**Call recording consent.** California requires consent from all parties to record a call. Make sure the greeting discloses that the call is automated and recorded if the tool records. Ask your lawyer if you are unsure.

## When a cheaper option is better

You may not need a full AI voice agent. Consider the simpler options first.

- **Online booking only.** If most of your clients already book through an app or link, push that harder. Put the link in your Instagram bio, Google Business Profile and voicemail greeting.
- **Missed-call text-back.** A text that says you will reply shortly, with a booking link, recovers many lost callers for a few dollars a month. Several phone apps include it.
- **A part-time human.** If your clients are older, high-end, or expect a personal voice, a human at the desk for peak hours may suit you better.
- **A live answering service.** CloudTalk lists human services from about $300 a month for 30 calls up to $2,100 for 300 calls on Smith.ai's live tiers. That is pricey per call, but it suits sensitive or complex enquiries.

A solo stylist who rents a booth and gets five calls a week probably does not need anything beyond text-back and online booking. A six-chair salon or a med spa with several providers and a busy phone is a much stronger fit.

## How to decide and what to do next

Run through this checklist before you commit.

1. Count your missed calls from the last 30 days and estimate their value.
2. Confirm which booking software you use and whether the vendor connects to it.
3. Ask for a trial or a month-to-month plan. Avoid long contracts for your first tool.
4. Test it with at least 15 realistic calls.
5. Set a review date 30 days after launch. Compare booked appointments from calls, missed calls, and client complaints against the previous month.

Keep a person in the loop for the first few weeks. Read the call summaries or transcripts every day, fix the wrong answers, and update the service menu as you go. Most early errors come from incomplete setup, not the technology.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does an AI receptionist for a salon cost?

Expect **$25 to $400 a month** for a single location. Basic plans with a small call allowance start around $25 to $80, and plans that handle phone and chat with full booking run $200 to $400. Check overage charges, because busy months can add to the bill.

### Can an AI receptionist replace my front desk?

For phone calls, often yes. It can answer, book and send confirmations. It cannot greet clients at the door, take payments in person, handle a distressed client or manage the floor. Most salons use it to cover the phone while staff focus on the people in the chair.

### Is an AI receptionist safe for a med spa?

It can be, if you choose carefully. Ask whether the vendor will sign a business associate agreement, where recordings are stored, and how long they are kept. Configure it so it never gives medical advice and always routes clinical questions to a qualified person.
