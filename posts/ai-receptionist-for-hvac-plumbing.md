## What an AI receptionist costs for an HVAC or plumbing company

An AI receptionist for contractors typically costs **about $30 to $300 a month** on published plans for small shops. Higher-volume or more customised setups cost more. Human-assisted services start higher, around $165 a month.

Those figures come from a 2026 roundup published by JustCall, a phone software vendor, so treat it as a guide and not a neutral survey. It lists Rosie from $49 a month for 250 minutes, with a $149 plan for 1,000 minutes and a $299 plan for 2,000 minutes. It lists My AI Front Desk at $79 a month billed annually, Dialzar at $29 a month, and Abby Connect from $165 a month for a service with a person on the line. Prices change often, so check each vendor's current page.

The plan price is not the whole bill. Setup, a connection to your scheduling software, and overage minutes can all add cost.

## What the AI actually does when a customer calls

An AI receptionist answers the phone in a natural-sounding voice. It asks what is wrong, collects the address and callback number, and either books a visit or passes the call to you. Most can also send a text confirmation.

For an HVAC or plumbing shop, a good setup handles these calls:

- **No heat or no cooling:** it asks how long, whether anyone is vulnerable at home, and flags the call as urgent.
- **Burst pipe or active leak:** it tells the caller to shut off the main valve if they can, then transfers to your on-call tech.
- **Quote and estimate requests:** it books a time slot and records the equipment type and age.
- **Maintenance and tune-ups:** it books these straight into open slots.
- **Existing customers:** it takes a message or passes them to the office.

## Why missed calls matter more in the trades

A homeowner with water on the kitchen floor does not leave a voicemail. They call the next company on the list. Emergency repair work is also your most profitable work, so a missed call there costs more than a missed call in most trades.

Here is a simple way to estimate your own loss. This is an illustration, not a benchmark. Suppose you miss 15 calls a month, and a third of them would have become jobs at an average ticket of $450. That is five jobs and about $2,250 in lost revenue a month. Swap in your own numbers from your call log.

If your phone shows you cannot answer calls on roofs, in crawl spaces and at night, the gap is probably larger than you think. Check your missed-call history for a few weeks before you buy anything.

Staffing is part of the picture too. JustCall cites Bureau of Labor Statistics projections of roughly 40,600 HVAC and 42,000 plumbing openings a year through 2035. Hiring a dedicated dispatcher is not easy, and you pay for them whether the phone rings or not.

## AI receptionist vs voicemail vs live answering service vs hiring

The right option depends on your call volume and how much of it arrives outside working hours. Here is how the four choices compare in practice.

- **Voicemail:** free, and fine if you only get a few calls a week. Many emergency callers hang up without leaving a message.
- **AI receptionist:** roughly $30 to $300 a month on the published plans above. It answers instantly, around the clock, and handles several calls at once. It is weaker with unusual situations.
- **Live answering service:** a human reads a script and takes messages. Pricing often runs per minute or per call, and the quality depends on the operator knowing your trade.
- **In-house dispatcher or office admin:** the best judgement and the highest cost. Salary, benefits and sick days all apply, and you still have a gap after hours.

Many shops use a mix. The office answers during the day, and AI picks up overflow, lunch breaks and nights.

## Where an AI receptionist is a poor fit

Some businesses should not buy one yet. Be honest about which group you are in.

- **You get fewer than a handful of calls a week.** A good voicemail greeting and a text-back may be enough.
- **Your calls are mostly complex commercial work.** Facility managers and general contractors often want to talk to a named person who knows the account.
- **You have no clear rules for urgent jobs.** The AI only follows the rules you give it. If you cannot say what counts as an emergency, it cannot either.
- **Your callers are mostly older customers who dislike automated systems.** Test this with a few real calls before you commit.

AI also makes mistakes. It can mishear a street name, misjudge urgency, or book a slot your crew cannot reach. Plan for a human to review bookings each morning, at least in the first month.

## What to check before you buy

Ask every vendor the same questions, and ask for a live demo using your own scenarios.

- **Scheduling integration.** Does it connect to ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro or Google Calendar? JustCall lists native ServiceTitan and Jobber logging for its product. If your software is not supported, bookings arrive as emails you must key in by hand.
- **Emergency transfer.** Can it call your on-call tech's mobile and fall back to a second number if there is no answer?
- **Service area and pricing rules.** Can it refuse jobs outside your zip codes and avoid quoting prices you have not approved?
- **Minute limits and overage fees.** A 250-minute plan sounds generous until you work out your average call length. At four minutes a call, that is about 62 calls.
- **Call recordings and transcripts.** You should be able to listen to any call that went wrong.
- **Language support.** In California and much of the US, Spanish-speaking callers are a large share of the market. Dialzar, for example, lists Spanish on every tier, so confirm what you will actually get.
- **Contract terms.** Prefer monthly billing for the first few months, even if annual billing is cheaper.

## Legal points to know, especially for callbacks

Answering inbound calls with AI is the low-risk use. Calling people back or calling out is where the rules tighten.

The FCC ruled on February 8, 2024 that calls using AI-generated voices count as "artificial or prerecorded voice" under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act. The FCC's own summary says these calls need the prior express consent of the person called. Law firm commentary puts statutory damages at $500 to $1,500 per call for violations, according to Henson Legal.

In plain terms, do not set your AI to cold-call homeowners or to run marketing calls to old customer lists without documented consent. Ask your vendor how it handles consent, opt-outs and disclosure that the caller is an AI. Also check California rules on call recording, since many states require all parties to be told a call is recorded. Speak to a lawyer about your own setup, because this post is not legal advice.

## How to set one up in about two weeks

A sensible rollout does not need to take long. It does need some preparation.

1. **Write down your call types.** List the ten reasons people call, and the correct response to each one.
2. **Define "emergency".** Be specific: no heat below a set outside temperature, active leaks, gas smell, sewage backup.
3. **Set your on-call routing.** Include a backup number and what the AI should say if nobody answers.
4. **Load your service area, hours and approved information.** Leave out prices unless you are sure of them.
5. **Test with 20 real calls.** Have staff and friends call with awkward questions, accents and background noise.
6. **Go live after hours first.** Then extend to overflow and lunch, and only later to full daytime coverage if it performs.
7. **Review weekly.** Read transcripts, fix the rules, and track booked jobs against missed calls.

## FAQ

### How much does an AI receptionist cost for a plumber?

Published plans for small shops start at about $29 to $79 a month and go to roughly $299 a month for 2,000 minutes, based on the 2026 JustCall roundup. A service with live human help starts around $165 a month. Your real cost depends on call volume, minutes used and any software integration.

### Can an AI receptionist book jobs into ServiceTitan or Jobber?

Some can. Support varies by vendor and plan, so confirm the exact integration and ask to see a test booking appear in your system before you sign.

### Will customers know they are talking to AI?

Many will notice, and some states and proposed FCC rules point toward disclosure. It is better to tell callers up front. Most people accept it when the call ends with a booked appointment or a tech on the way.

## How to decide

If you miss calls after hours, during jobs or at lunch, and each lost call costs you hundreds of dollars, an AI receptionist is likely to pay for itself within a month or two. If you rarely miss calls, a better voicemail greeting costs less.

Start with a month-to-month plan, run it after hours first, and judge it on one number: how many extra jobs it booked.
