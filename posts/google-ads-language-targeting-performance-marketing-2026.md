## What is changing in Google Ads language targeting?

Google Ads is changing how advertisers manage language in Search and Performance Max campaigns. MediaPost reported on September 11, 2026, that campaign-level language targeting will disappear from those campaign types starting in late September. The platform will instead place more emphasis on the language of the search term, the ad creative, and the landing page when deciding which eligible ad is most relevant.

Google Ads product liaison Ginny Marvin told MediaPost that the system already considers signals such as language preferences, ad creative, landing-page language, and search-term language. The update adds enhanced ad-group prioritization when multiple eligible campaigns or ad groups use different languages.

This is important for **performance marketing teams running multilingual campaigns**. The change does not mean advertisers must translate every ad or rebuild every account. It does mean that language strategy is moving further away from a single campaign setting and closer to the quality, organization, and relevance of the assets available at auction time.

MediaPost also reported that language exclusions are not supported today, and that leaving a language unselected does not exclude it. Google said reporting will not change, and that the update does not translate ad creative. Advertisers using single-language campaigns, such as English or Spanish, should not see a fundamental change simply because of this update.

## Why creative and landing-page language matter more

When campaign-level language targeting is no longer the main control, the ad and destination page have to communicate clearly. A user searching in German is more likely to receive priority for an ad and website written in German when those assets are eligible and relevant. The same principle applies to other languages.

For a multilingual account, language alignment should be visible at every step:

- The search term or keyword theme reflects the intended market.
- The ad headline and description use the intended language naturally.
- Sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets, and assets match the same language.
- The landing page headline, body copy, form, pricing, and calls to action are translated consistently.
- Confirmation pages and follow-up messages do not switch languages unexpectedly.

A translation alone is not enough. Regional vocabulary, currency, delivery expectations, legal information, and customer-support details also influence whether a visitor can act. A campaign may receive a click but fail to convert if the landing page feels incomplete or inconsistent.

Create a language matrix for every market. Record the language, country or region, ad group, landing-page URL, offer, conversion action, and owner responsible for updates. This makes gaps visible before an account is expanded.

## Audit existing Google Ads campaigns before the update

Google said there is no need to restructure campaigns, but advertisers should review their setup. An audit can help distinguish what must change from what can remain as it is.

Start with a campaign and ad-group inventory containing:

- Campaign type and objective.
- Geographic locations and excluded locations.
- Ad-group themes and keyword coverage.
- Asset language and landing-page language.
- Conversion actions and attribution settings.
- Budget, bidding strategy, and recent change history.
- Whether different languages compete inside the same campaign or account area.

Next, identify accounts where several language versions are eligible for the same search context. Review whether the ad groups are easy to understand and whether each one has a clearly matched destination. If a single ad group mixes languages, offers, or audiences, consider whether that structure makes testing and reporting harder even if Google does not require a rebuild.

Do not make large structural changes only because a setting is being removed. Document the reason for every change and compare results before and after the update. Performance marketing works best when the team can connect an edit to an observable outcome.

## Build language-aligned ad groups and assets

Google’s update makes relevance between a user’s context and an advertiser’s language assets more important. A clean structure gives the system more useful options and gives the marketing team clearer diagnostics.

For each language, create a consistent asset set where appropriate:

- A focused ad group with a specific search intent.
- Headlines and descriptions written or reviewed by a fluent speaker.
- Language-matched sitelinks and callouts.
- A landing page that keeps the same offer and terminology.
- Conversion tracking that records the language and market when useful.

Avoid literal translations that change the promise or make the copy sound unnatural. The best workflow combines a translation or drafting tool with human review from someone familiar with the audience. The reviewer should check headlines, disclaimers, currencies, dates, forms, and the words customers actually use when searching.

Keep the offer consistent unless the market genuinely requires a different product, price, or process. If markets differ, record those differences in the brief. This helps analysts avoid comparing campaigns that are not designed to achieve the same outcome.

## Review landing pages, forms, and conversion paths

A language-matched ad can still underperform when the post-click experience is not ready. Review the full journey, not just the first page.

Check each localized experience for:

- Headline and offer matching the ad.
- Navigation and buttons in the same language.
- Form labels, validation messages, and error states translated.
- Privacy notices and consent language presented appropriately.
- Phone numbers, addresses, currency, and business hours relevant to the market.
- Confirmation pages and email or SMS follow-up in the expected language.
- Mobile layout, page speed, and readable text after translation.
- Analytics events firing on the localized URL.

A landing page should make the next step obvious. If a user clicks a German ad and reaches an English form, the mismatch may reduce trust even if the product is suitable. The same problem appears when a translated page contains an untranslated navigation menu or a call-to-action that leads to a different language.

Use a test submission for every active language version. Record the page, form, confirmation, notification, and CRM entry so the team can verify that the conversion path works end to end.

## What to do with Google Ads API workflows

The MediaPost report quoted Google’s recommendation to stop including language criteria in new and updated Search campaigns when sending Google Ads API requests. For existing campaigns, Google suggested that advertisers may remove language targeting for hygiene purposes because the setting will no longer apply.

Teams using an internal campaign builder or automation script should review the workflow before the late-September change. Look for:

- Code that sends language criteria when creating or updating Search campaigns.
- Validation rules that treat language targeting as mandatory.
- Reporting dashboards that expect the campaign setting to determine language.
- Templates that create mixed-language ad groups without a clear mapping.
- Bulk-edit scripts that could remove or alter unrelated settings.

Test API changes in a controlled environment and keep a log of requests. Do not delete historical performance data simply because a targeting field is changing. The objective is to make future campaign operations compatible while preserving useful reporting and account history.

If an agency manages the account, ask for a written explanation of how its tools will handle the transition. The request should cover new campaign creation, existing-campaign cleanup, asset selection, and reporting—not just whether a checkbox is still visible.

## Measurement after campaign-level language targeting disappears

Google reported that reporting will not change, but performance marketers should still add language context to their own analysis. Campaign-level reporting may not answer every question about which language experience drove a result.

Useful dimensions can include:

- Ad group and asset language.
- Landing-page language.
- Country or region.
- Search term language where available and appropriate.
- Conversion action and value.
- Device and placement.
- New versus returning customer status when tracked lawfully.

Set a baseline before the change using the metrics that matter to the business. Depending on the objective, this could include qualified leads, completed purchases, booked calls, revenue, cost per acquisition, or return on ad spend. Do not treat a lower click cost as success if the leads are not useful.

Annotate the account when language settings are removed or campaigns are edited. Compare like with like, and allow enough time for normal auction and conversion variation before drawing a conclusion. Performance marketing decisions should be based on trends and business outcomes rather than one day of data.

## A practical Google Ads language targeting checklist

Use this checklist before the change takes effect:

- List every active language, market, campaign, ad group, and landing page.
- Confirm that ad assets and landing pages use the intended language.
- Review mixed-language campaigns and document why each version is eligible.
- Test forms, confirmation pages, and follow-up messages.
- Ask fluent reviewers to check translations and commercial claims.
- Update API templates so new campaigns do not depend on the retiring criterion.
- Preserve historical data and annotate structural or setting changes.
- Add language and landing-page context to internal reports where possible.
- Confirm that conversion tracking works for every localized path.
- Monitor qualified outcomes after the late-September rollout.

The goal is not to create unnecessary complexity. It is to make the account’s available assets clear, relevant, and useful for the language context in which a person searches.

## FAQ: Google Ads language targeting in 2026

### Is Google removing language targeting from all Google Ads campaigns?

The reported change applies to campaign-level language targeting in Google Search and Performance Max campaigns starting in late September 2026. Review Google’s current documentation and account notices for the latest scope.

### Does this mean Google will translate my ads automatically?

No. MediaPost reported that Google does not translate ad creative. Advertisers remain responsible for creating and reviewing language-appropriate ads and landing pages.

### Do I need to restructure every multilingual campaign?

No. Google said there is no need to restructure campaigns. An audit is still useful to confirm that language versions, ad groups, landing pages, and conversion paths are clearly organized.

### Will my reporting change?

MediaPost reported that reporting will not change. Businesses may still want internal reporting dimensions for ad language, landing-page language, market, and conversion quality.

### What should API users do first?

Review templates and automation that send language criteria for new or updated Search campaigns. Test changes carefully, and avoid broad edits that are not required for the transition.
