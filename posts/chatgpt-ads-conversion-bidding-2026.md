## A New Advertising Platform Grows Up

For most of 2026, performance marketers treated ChatGPT Ads as an experiment — interesting traffic, but thin on the optimization tools that Google Ads and Meta Ads advertisers rely on. That changed on July 27, 2026, when ChatGPT Ads rolled out a batch of features aimed squarely at performance marketers: conversion-optimized bidding, mobile measurement integrations, automatic advanced matching, rolling daily budgets, and asynchronous bulk campaign management through the Ads API.

The update does not introduce a flashy new ad format. Instead, it adds the foundational capabilities that make a platform viable for serious ad spend — conversion tracking, attribution, budget pacing, and scalable campaign creation. For brands already running paid campaigns on Google and Meta, the question is no longer whether ChatGPT Ads exists, but whether it deserves a line item in the budget.

## Conversion-Optimized Bidding Arrives

The headline feature is **conversion-optimized cost-per-click (oCPC) campaigns**. Advertisers can now select a Conversions objective that automatically optimizes for clicks most likely to lead to conversions, while still maintaining CPC pricing. This is the same logic that made Meta's conversion campaigns and Google's Smart Bidding powerful — the platform learns which clicks convert and bids accordingly.

Why this matters: until now, ChatGPT Ads advertisers were optimizing for clicks, not outcomes. That is fine for awareness, but performance marketing lives and dies by cost-per-acquisition (CPA) and return on ad spend (ROAS). With oCPC, advertisers can set a conversion goal and let the platform optimize toward it, which is the basic requirement for any platform to compete for performance budgets.

The platform still uses CPC pricing, which means advertisers pay per click — but the bidding engine now weights those clicks by conversion likelihood. This is a meaningful shift for anyone who has been hesitant to test ChatGPT Ads because they could not tie spend to outcomes.

## Stronger Measurement and Attribution

Conversion bidding only works if the platform can measure conversions accurately. The July 2026 update delivers on that front with two key additions:

- **AppsFlyer and Adjust integrations**: Advertisers can now measure app installs and in-app events driven by ChatGPT Ads campaigns. This matters for mobile-first brands and app developers who previously had a blind spot in their attribution stack.
- **Automatic Advanced Matching**: Uses hashed customer data to improve website conversion attribution. This is the same approach Meta and Google use to match ad clicks to website conversions when third-party cookies are unavailable, which is increasingly the norm in 2026.

Together, these features close a measurement gap that kept data-driven advertisers away. If you can trust the conversion data, you can trust the bidding — and that is the foundation of performance marketing on any platform.

## Budget Management and Campaign Automation

Performance marketers running campaigns at scale need more than good bidding — they need efficient campaign management. The update adds several tools that advertisers have come to expect from mature platforms:

- **Rolling seven-day average daily budgets**: Campaigns now use average daily budgets calculated over a rolling seven-day period. Daily spend can fluctuate, but the overall budget stays intact, with automatic budget pacing distributing spend more evenly throughout the day.
- **Geographic exclusions**: Advertisers can remove specific locations from targeting, giving finer control over where ads appear.
- **Asynchronous bulk creation and updates**: Through the Ads API, advertisers can create and update campaigns, ad groups, and ads in bulk — essential for managing large accounts or feeding creative from an automated pipeline.
- **Updated product feed cards**: Product feed campaigns now display product cards with pricing and star ratings, improving the retail ad experience.

These are not glamorous features, but they are the difference between a platform you can run one test campaign on and one you can operate at scale. Bulk API access in particular opens the door to automation — feed product data, generate creative variants, and push updates programmatically, the same way performance teams operate on Google and Meta.

## Where ChatGPT Ads Fits in the 2026 Mix

The honest framing is that ChatGPT Ads is still early. The July 2026 update is about reaching feature parity with the basics, not surpassing Google or Meta. But that parity matters. Performance marketing is a portfolio game — you allocate budget across channels based on where you can acquire customers profitably. A new channel that offers:

- A distinct audience (people in a conversational, intent-rich context)
- Conversion-optimized bidding
- Proper measurement integrations
- API-driven campaign management

...is a channel worth testing. The brands that get in early, build creative tailored to the conversational format, and feed conversion data back into the oCPC engine will have a learning advantage over those who wait for the platform to be "proven."

The risk is treating ChatGPT Ads like a Google Ads clone. The ad experience is different — users are in a chat context, not a search results page or a social feed. Creative and offers that work on Google may not translate directly. Plan for platform-specific creative testing from day one.

**Does ChatGPT Ads support bulk campaign management?**

**Is ChatGPT Ads ready to replace Google or Meta?**
No — and it is not trying to yet. The 2026 updates bring it to feature parity on the fundamentals (conversion bidding, measurement, automation), making it a viable test channel alongside Google and Meta rather than a replacement.
