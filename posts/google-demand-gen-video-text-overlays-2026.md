## What changed in Google Demand Gen video ads

Google Ads is introducing an **“Overlay on videos”** asset-optimization setting for Demand Gen campaigns, according to [Search Engine Land’s October 9 report](https://searchengineland.com/google-ads-adds-automatic-text-overlays-to-demand-gen-video-ads-494885). When enabled, Google can use an advertiser’s existing text assets to generate messages displayed over video creatives. The setting appears alongside options for shorter and resized videos.

The practical change is that the video shown in an ad may include text treatment in addition to the advertiser’s original edit. That may suit a campaign where a concise benefit or offer needs to be visible while a viewer watches without sound. It can also alter a carefully designed frame, so advertisers should inspect the setting and the served creative rather than assume the uploaded video will always appear unchanged.

Search Engine Land reports that the option appeared enabled by default in its observation. Because account interfaces and feature availability can change, check your own campaign settings instead of assuming the toggle is either on or off everywhere.

## How automatic overlays use your text assets

The reported feature draws from text assets already supplied by the advertiser and places generated text on video ads. That makes existing copy more important: headlines, descriptions, and other campaign text may influence what appears over the video.

Before enabling or retaining the option, review those assets as if they might be used as on-screen copy. Check that they are:

- **Accurate:** claims, product details, prices, and offers match the landing page and current campaign.
- **Clear on their own:** a short message should still make sense when separated from its original headline or description context.
- **Consistent:** terminology and tone should match the video, brand, and destination page.
- **Current:** remove expired promotions, obsolete product details, or messaging that no longer applies.
- **Appropriate for the audience:** avoid wording that could be misunderstood when displayed without surrounding copy.

These checks are useful for any automated creative feature: systems can reuse submitted assets, but the advertiser remains responsible for choosing the source material and evaluating whether the result represents the offer correctly.

## Inspect the setting before a campaign runs

Open the relevant Demand Gen campaign and review its **Asset optimization** preferences. Look for “Overlay on videos” and confirm whether you want it enabled for that campaign. Search Engine Land reports that advertisers can switch off the overlay option individually or use “Disable all” to turn off the available video optimization features.

A careful review sequence can help:

1. Open the campaign’s asset optimization settings.
2. Confirm the current state of “Overlay on videos”; do not rely on a previous campaign’s preferences.
3. Decide whether your creative is designed to accommodate additional text.
4. If you disable overlays, confirm the change is saved before assuming the asset will remain untouched.
5. Preview the available ad combinations and check the live or eligible version when possible.
6. Revisit the setting when you add new videos or update campaign text assets.

The settings path and available controls may evolve. Use the controls shown in your account, and verify the change at the campaign level, especially when multiple campaigns have different brand or legal requirements.

## Protect the composition of your video creative

Automatic text can be helpful only when it does not obscure what viewers need to see. A line of copy placed over a product, a face, a subtitle, or an important visual cue may weaken the message. This is especially relevant when your video already includes designed-in text, captions, offer details, or a carefully arranged end card.

Evaluate overlays against the actual frame, not only against the copy in a spreadsheet. Review key moments throughout the video, including opening seconds, product demonstrations, close-ups, and calls to action. Check for:

- Text covering a product feature or a person's face.
- Competing messages from designed-in captions and the generated overlay.
- Low contrast or small text against a busy background.
- A promotion shown without its conditions or expiry context.
- A call to action that conflicts with the destination page.

If you plan to test the feature, use video assets with enough visual space for a message and avoid placing critical information where an overlay might cover it. Keep an untouched version of the original creative so the team can compare it with an optimized variant.

## Check labels and market-specific requirements

Search Engine Land says Google notes that ads using image or video assets modified by these optimization features are labeled as created or edited with AI when served in the European Union, India, and New York state. Check the current platform documentation and the account’s served-asset information for the markets where your campaign runs; do not assume that a label, control, or policy works identically in every region.

This is a campaign-review consideration, not legal advice. Teams should confirm their applicable advertising and disclosure obligations, particularly where the creative contains a regulated product, a sensitive claim, or a promotion with important conditions. A platform-provided label does not replace a marketer’s own review of claims, permissions, and brand standards.

## A simple test plan for Demand Gen overlays

Treat **Google Demand Gen video text overlays** as a creative variable to evaluate, not a guaranteed performance improvement. The Search Engine Land report notes possible benefits such as reinforcing a product benefit or call to action, including for viewers who watch without sound; it also notes possible interference with the existing design. Your own account results and creative review should guide the decision.

For a controlled evaluation:

- Choose a video where a short overlay could add context rather than repeat all of the existing copy.
- Save the original version and document the campaign setting before making a change.
- Review the rendered ad for accuracy, legibility, composition, and brand consistency.
- Compare the result using the campaign's existing measurement plan and conversion definitions.
- Check that any observed difference is not explained by other changes made at the same time.
- Keep the overlay only if it meets both the creative standard and the business objective.

Do not judge the treatment from a single screenshot if the campaign can serve different assets or placements. Capture what version was served and review it in context. If the overlay adds no useful information or disrupts the core visual, switch it off for that campaign and retain control of the original edit.

## FAQ: Google Demand Gen video text overlays

### What are Google Demand Gen video text overlays?

They are messages that Google can generate from an advertiser’s existing text assets and display over video ads when the “Overlay on videos” optimization is enabled, according to Search Engine Land’s report.

### Is the overlay setting enabled by default?

Search Engine Land reported that it appeared enabled by default in its observation. Check the current setting in your own campaign because availability and account configuration can vary.

### Can advertisers turn off overlays without disabling every video optimization?

The report says advertisers can switch off “Overlay on videos” individually or choose “Disable all” for the available video optimization features. Verify the controls in your account before making changes.

### Will overlays affect every market in the same way?

Not necessarily. Search Engine Land reports that Google identifies AI-related labeling for modified image or video assets served in the EU, India, and New York state. Review current documentation and requirements for each campaign market.
